# Retail Algorithmic / Directional Trading Economics in India (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: what a retail individual trading daily-directional ideas on NSE cash equity (delivery, long-only) or F&O can keep after all costs and frictions; what the rules allow (SEBI/exchanges, 2024→2026); and what trustworthy performance records exist. All rupee figures in INR. Rates were checked against the live Zerodha charges page and the SEBI/Aug-2026 study on 7 Nov 2026.

## Key Question 1: Full per-round-trip cost stack — delivery, intraday/BTST, F&O — and realistic slippage

### Takeaway
Statutory + broker charges make a cash-delivery round trip cost ~0.24% of trade value before slippage (~₹238 per ₹1 lakh traded, with ₹0 brokerage at Zerodha), and an intraday round trip ~0.10–0.11%; the dominant single cost on delivery is STT (0.1% each side). STT on futures (0.05%) and options sell-side premium (0.15%) was raised again from 1 April 2026 (Budget 2026), and slippage of roughly 5–20 bps per side on liquid large caps is well-documented — so a daily-traded delivery idea must clear ~0.35–0.65% per round trip before it nets a rupee.

### Cited Findings

**Delivery (cash equity, NSE) — current published schedule (Zerodha page fetched 7 Nov 2026):**
- Brokerage: ₹0 on equity delivery (discount broker) — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- STT: 0.1% (₹100/lakh) on both buy and sell sides of delivery — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/); STT table — [Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated)
- Exchange transaction charges (cash): NSE 0.00307% per side; BSE 0.00375% per side — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- SEBI turnover fee: ₹10 per crore; GST: 18% on (brokerage + SEBI fee + transaction charges); NSE IPFT: ₹0.01 per crore — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- Stamp duty: 0.015% (₹1,500/cr) on buy side (delivery) — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- DP (depository) charges: ₹15.34 per scrip (₹3.5 CDSL + ₹9.5 Zerodha + ₹2.34 GST), charged on the sell-only debits "irrespective of quantity" — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/) (other brokers typically ₹20+/scrip; e.g. comparesharebrokers lists Zerodha "₹15.34 + GST" — [comparesharebrokers](https://comparesharebrokers.com/brokerage-charges/zerodha))

**Worked delivery example (₹1,00,000 bought and sold, Zerodha, NSE):** STT ₹200.00; txn ₹6.14; SEBI fee ₹0.02; stamp ₹15.00; GST ₹1.11; DP ₹15.34 → **≈ ₹237.6 per round trip ≈ 0.238% of RT value** (≈ ₹222 without DP; traditional full-service brokers add ~0.1–0.5% per leg on top). Illustrative arithmetic computed from the rate table above, cross-checkable in — [Zerodha brokerage calculator](https://zerodha.com/brokerage-calculator/)

**Intraday (mis/BTST-style same-day or T+1/T+2):**
- Brokerage: 0.03% or ₹20 per executed order, whichever lower, each side (Zerodha) — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- STT: only 0.025% on the sell side — [Zerodha support STT table](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated)
- Stamp duty: 0.003% (₹300/cr) buy side; transaction charges same as delivery (NSE 0.00307%); DP charge generally not on intraday but **does apply on delivery-settled BTST flows where shares are debited** — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/) (BTST DP-treatment not fully spelled out on the page; also check — [Charges explained](https://zerodha.com/charges/))
- Worked intraday example (₹1L RT): brokerage ₹60 (0.03%×2) + STT ₹25 + txn ₹6.14 + stamp ₹3 + SEBI ₹0.02 + GST ₹11.90 = **≈ ₹106 ≈ 0.106% per RT** (≈ ₹82 if brokerage hits the ₹20/order cap). [Derived from — Zerodha charges/calculator]

**F&O (rates in force 1 Apr 2026, per Budget 2026 revision):**
- STT was revised from 1 April 2026: **futures 0.05% on sell side (was 0.02% from 1 Oct 2024); options sell-side premium 0.15% (was 0.1% from 1 Oct 2024); options bought-and-exercised 0.15% of intrinsic value** — [Zerodha bulletin "Revision in STT from 1st April 2026"](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/445377/revision-in-stt-securities-transaction-tax-from-1st-april-2026); confirmed on live — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/); "options STT 0.15% per Budget 2026" — [Tradetron State of Retail Algo Trading](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- Options STT history: 0.0625% → 0.1% on 1 Oct 2024 (Budget July 2024), → 0.15% on 1 Apr 2026 — [Zerodha support STT page](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated); [Zerodha bulletin](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/445377/revision-in-stt-securities-transaction-tax-from-1st-april-2026)
- Transaction charges: options NSE 0.03553% on premium (BSE 0.0325%), futures NSE 0.00183% (BSE ₹0); stamp: futures 0.002% buy, options 0.003% buy — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- NIFTY option round-trip, worked May-2026-rates table (1 lot = 65, ₹150 premium): brokerage ₹10 (deep-discount ₹5/lot), STT ₹14.63, txn ₹6.83, GST ₹3.03, stamp+SEBI ₹0.31 → visible ≈ **₹34.80**, ≈₹32.50 slippage → **realistic ≈ ₹67/RT**; at 1 RT/day ≈ ₹16,000/yr, 3/day ≈ ₹47,000/yr, 5/day ≈ ₹79,000 (235 trading days) — [Tradetron research report](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- Contract-size context: NIFTY lot revised to 65 per "NSE's January-2026 revision" — [Tradetron report](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html); SEBI's Oct-2024 reset moved min contract size to ₹15–20 lakh (lots revised for new contracts from 20 Nov 2024) — [SEBI F&O Oct-2024 framework summary](https://v2.webnotes.in/sebi-fno-entry-barrier-rules-2024)
- MTF (margin/leveraged delivery): Zerodha interest 0.04%/day (₹40/lakh) + 0.3%-or-₹20 brokerage on MTF trades; pledging ₹30/ISIN — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)

**Slippage / market impact (NIFTY-100 large caps, evening-signal → next-open-like execution):**
- NSE's own impact-cost framework: a Nifty 50 constituent must show impact cost ≤0.50% on a ₹10 crore basket for 90% of observations (i.e., survey-sized ₹10cr orders in ultra-liquid names are bounded at ~50 bps); impact cost is "the cost of executing a transaction in a given stock, for a specific predefined order size" — [NSE impact-cost page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/indices-impact-cost); NIFTY/50-eligibility norm quoted — [Rupeecase trading-costs module](https://www.rupeecase.com/learn/path-10/module-10-2-the-real-cost-of-trading-slippage-and-transaction-costs)
- Research summarized for Indian algos: average slippage ~5–20 bps per trade (0.05–0.20%), worse in event volatility — [Bigul (bank-backed broker) slippage guide](https://bigul.co/blog/algo-trading/navigating-slippage-in-algo-trading-your-guide-to-smoother-execution-in-india); practitioner cost guides treat ~0.25/lot slippage per side on NIFTY options as "tight execution" — [Tradetron report](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- A 2025 methods paper builds cost-aware backtesting over 15 years of daily data on 25 Indian large-caps specifically to capture indicator-limits + transaction-cost decay — [Anchan et al., Authorea 2025](https://www.authorea.com/doi/10.22541/au.176120687.73846026)
- Tradetron's research chapter: on 200+ liquid F&O names over 6.3 years of minute data, "every step faster (1-min vs 15-min charts) added whipsaw and costs and removed nothing but profit" (₹8,094 vs ₹61,457 for the identical rule) — i.e., short-horizon edges are eaten by costs/liquidity — [Tradetron report, Ch. 3 & 5](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)

### Inferences
- Per-round-trip retained edge rule of thumb (delivery): a long-only daily-delivery idea needs ≥ ~0.25% visible costs + ~0.10–0.40% execution slippage (≥ ~0.35–0.65% total) per round trip just to break even; at daily cadence on ₹1 lakh that is ₹80k–1.5L/yr — i.e., the *turnover itself* typically exceeds any realistic gross edge, which is why the studies below show turnover (not strategy quality) as the main killer. At weekly/positional cadence the same costs are ~0.35–0.65%/yr of traded value — survivable.
- For next-open execution (signal generated on evening data, executed at/before next open), the price gap itself is alpha information; the *execution-only* slippage on top-NIFTY-100 names is plausibly in the low single digits to ~10 bps per side at retail size (≤₹10L orders), but rising to 20+ bps in auction imbalances/high-news days. No India-specific next-open execution study was found; treat this band as a reasoned estimate, not a documented number.
- The April-2026 STT hike (futures 0.02→0.05%; options 0.1→0.15%) roughly doubles derivatives STT again on top of the Oct-2024 hike, raising every F&O round trip materially; SEBI itself noted FY26 transaction costs stayed "broadly unchanged despite lower premium turnover because of the STT increase from Oct 1, 2024" — signalling regulators now treat STT as an *anti-churn instrument*, not just revenue.

### Gaps
- No India-published, order-size-specific slippage distribution for NIFTY-100 names (e.g., percentile impact cost by ticket size) was found; NSE publishes only the eligibility impact-cost norm and historical sample files.
- Intraday/BTST DP-charge treatment varies by broker and settlement path; Zerodha's page doesn't state the BTST case explicitly (verify with a specific broker before modelling).
- Full-service brokers' delivery brokerage ranges (0.1–0.5%) weren't re-verified for 2026 fee cards (many have also adopted discount-style ₹20 caps); only Zerodha's schedule was pulled line-by-line.

## Key Question 2: SEBI regulation 2024→2026 — F&O tightening, retail algo framework, tips/finfluencer crackdown

### Takeaway
Oct 2024 brought six F&O friction measures (min contract size ₹15–20L, one weekly expiry/exchange, upfront premium collection, expiry-day margins, no calendar-spread benefit expiry day, intraday position monitoring); from Sep 1, 2025 NSE/BSE expiries moved to Tue/Thu; NIFTY lot was cut to 65 in Jan 2026. The Feb 4, 2025 SEBI circular made retail API/algo trading a recognized, permitted activity — no exchange strategy registration needed below an order-rate threshold (initially ≤10 orders/sec), but brokers must whitelist a static IP and tag every algo order with an exchange-issued ID; selling algos to others requires broker empanelment (white-box, transparent) or an RA license (black-box). A Jan 29, 2025 circular and the Sep-2024 amendments bar SEBI-registered entities from associating with unregistered finfluencers/tips providers.

### Cited Findings
- **Six F&O measures announced 1 Oct 2024** (effective Nov 2024–Apr 2025): higher minimum contract size ₹15–20 lakh; **weekly expiries capped at one per exchange**; increased expiry-day tail/ELM margins; upfront collection of option premium (from buyers); removal of calendar-spread margin benefit on expiry day; intraday position-limit monitoring in index options — [SEBI F&O Oct 2024 explainer](https://v2.webnotes.in/sebi-fno-entry-barrier-rules-2024); [ET explainer](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/fo-clampdown-how-do-sebis-new-rules-affect-traders-and-brokers/articleshow/113874035.cms); timeline through Apr 2026 incl. "STT and position-limit calculation" changes — [Alice Blue](https://aliceblueonline.com/blog/sebi-new-fo-rules-2024-to-2026)
- **Effect measurement** (SEBI comparative study, Dec 2024–May 2025): index options notional turnover −29% YoY (premium terms −9%); unique individual F&O traders fell ~20% YoY between Dec 2024 and May 2025 — [SEBI study PDF Jul 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2025/1751900271726.pdf); [Gale.in summary](https://gale.in/sebi-fo-rules-explained/)
- **Expiry-day migration:** since 1 Sep 2025, NSE index derivatives expire on **Tuesday**, BSE on **Thursday** — [Accelpix](https://accelpix.com/blog/expiry-day-data-what-changes/); [HDFC Sec](https://www.hdfcsec.com/blog/details/revision-of-nse-bse-expiry-day-of-derivatives-contracts); [Groww explainer](https://groww.in/blog/sebi-nod-nse-to-shift-weekly-f-and-o-expiry-to-tuesday-bse-gets-thursday); Zerodha: weekly expiries now only on Nifty 50 (Bank Nifty/FinNifty/Midcap Select weekly discontinued) — [Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/trading-faqs/f-otrading/articles/weekly-expiries-discontinued-for-certain-index-derivatives)
- **Retail algo framework — SEBI circular 4 Feb 2025 (SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000013,** "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading")**:** recognizes retail API/algo trading as legitimate; below an exchange-set order-rate threshold **no strategy registration is required**; algo must run from a **broker-whitelisted static IP**; every algo order tagged with an **exchange-issued unique algo ID**; brokers accountable for client-side algo compliance — [SEBI circular page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html); [Zerodha's explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations)
- **Order-rate threshold in practice:** NSE set the Threshold Order Per Second (TOPS) initially at **≤10 orders/second** (adjustable by exchanges) — [NSE circular INVG/67858](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG67858.pdf); Zerodha already enforced 10/sec on APIs and Kite — [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations)
- **Algo providers/marketplaces under the Feb-2025 framework:** algo vendors become **agents of brokers** and must be empanelled with exchanges; **white-box** (logic disclosed) algos register with an exchange once and can serve all users; **black-box** vendors need an SEBI **Research Analyst license**, must publish periodic strategy reports and inform the exchange of changes; all such orders carry the algo ID; individuals may share strategies **only with family members**; broker↔algo-provider **revenue sharing** (subscription + brokerage kickbacks) is expressly permitted subject to conflict-of-interest safeguards; a "Performance Validation Agency for Algos" role was contemplated — [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations); brokers/vendors marrying registration to SEBI via API — [AlgoDelta](https://algodelta.com/blog/sebi-circular-static-ip-algo-trading-registration-rules-explained/); vendors registering >10 orders/sec through a broker — [Marketcalls checklist](https://www.marketcalls.in/market-regulations/sebis-2025-regulations-for-retail-algo-trading-the-ultimate-compliance-checklist.html)
- **Timeline slippage:** original effectiveness 1 Aug 2025 → **extended to 1 Oct 2025** by SEBI circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/132 (Sep 2025) for a smooth broker transition — [SEBI extension circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/sep-2025/extension-of-timeline-for-implementation-of-sebi-circular-dated-february-04-2025-on-safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading-_96979.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-extends-deadline-for-retail-algo-trading-framework); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-pushes-algo-trading-deadline-to-october-1-2025); NSE implementation standards circular — [NSE/INVG/67858](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG67858.pdf); phasing recap for 2026 — [Tradetron explainer](https://tradetron.tech/blog/sebi-algo-trading-rules-in-india-2025)
- **Tips / unregistered advice / finfluencers:** SEBI amendments of Sep 2024 restricted SEBI-registered entities from associating with unregistered "finfluencers"; **circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-1/P/CIR/2025/11 (29 Jan 2025)** tightened this (registered intermediaries can't associate with unregistered persons creating investment advice content; termination of existing contracts within 3 months) — [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-sebi-regulated-entities-restriction-finfluencers/); [FinSec Law](https://www.finseclaw.com/article/sebi-directs-regulated-entities-to-terminate-contracts-with-unregulated-persons); [Legal 500 roundup](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/corporate-commercial-law/sebi's-crackdown-on-finfluencers-regulations-and-enforcement); 2025-26 state of the rules for advisers/finfluencers — [Dealplexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/sebi-finfluencer-rules)
- Continuation of the Feb-2025 framework into a live vendor regime was still being worked out in early 2026 (a registered NSE vendor described pending code-hosting requirements with Zerodha in a March 2026 comment) — [Zerodha z-connect comments](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations#comment-305545)

### Inferences
- Net position for an individual running **his/her own** code: in 2026 you can legally automate your own account with no strategy registration at standard retail rates (≤10 orders/sec threshold), but you must route via a compliant broker API, fixed/static IP, tagged algo ID; building a business around *other people's* money pulls you into RA licensing/exchange empanelment territory — effectively SEBI has converted "sell a strategy" from gray-zone tips into a licensed intermediation.
- The Sep-2024 finfluencer/tips rules + UA regime mean retail "punting on subscription tips" now mainly persists by evading SEBI's perimeter (unregistered Telegram groups), while the legitimate channels (registered broker partnerships, RA-licensed algos) must be performance-disclosed; enforcement against tips channels continued through 2024–25 per the finfluencer coverage above.
- Combined with STT hikes (Oct 2024; Apr 2026) and lot-size/contract-size resets, the regulatory design deliberately *prices out* high-frequency retail churn; the surviving low-cost regulatory lane for retail quant is exactly the low-turnover, positional style (see KQ4).

### Gaps
- The final published version of the broker-empanelment standards and the "Performance Validation Agency for Algos" operational rules were not located in published circulars (only the contemplated role in Zerodha's Feb-2025 explainer).
- Whether a further deadline extension occurred after 1 Oct 2025 (some vendor blog posts describe "phased-in" go-live into 2026) could not be confirmed from primary circulars.
- No verifiable list of SEBI-empanelled black-box algo vendors with published live track records was found.

## Key Question 3: Platform landscape — smallcase, Streak, Tradetron, AlgoTest, QuantMan, broker APIs — track records and fee models

### Takeaway
Almost no retail-facing platform publishes an independently verified, survivorship-adjusted live track record; the one platform that published a self-critical full-funnel ledger (Tradetron, Aug 2025–Aug 2026, 42,661 deployments/16,979 users) found only 29% of intraday and 53% of positional live deployments were even *gross*-profitable, with intraday winning rates and magnitudes not surviving costs once charges are added. Fee models are dominantly subscriptions (₹10–₹25/ISIN per day through per-trade and per-SIP plans like smallcase's ₹100/₹10), and the regulator has now explicitly legalized broker↔algo-provider subscription/kickback sharing (with an unaddressed conflict-of-interest problem), which materially biases whatever "performance" screens you see.

### Cited Findings
- **Tradetron "State of Retail Algo Trading in India" (First Edition, Aug 2026)** — platform's own aggregate ledger, all live automated deployments started Aug 2025–Aug 2026: **42,661 deployments by 16,979 users**; 32,893 with recorded positions analyzed; **29% of intraday vs 53% of positional deployments gross-positive** (median intraday P&L ₹0, median positional +₹679); per user, 27% intraday vs 45% positional gross-positive; restricted to genuinely used deployments (≥10 positions, ≥30 days) win rates converge 56–63% but magnitude gap persists (median active-used intraday ₹42–₹203 vs positional ₹5,796–₹8,440); **median intraday deployment was switched off after only 2 entries** (abandonment/churn); 1,026 same users running both styles showed 51% vs 54% gross-profitability with positional magnitudes ~2x; figures gross of charges; 9,768 no-position deployments excluded — [Tradetron research report](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- Tradetron's published cost chapter and research null results: "hundreds of thousands of backtests… nearly all produced nulls"; the four surviving return sources (market drift, cross-sectional momentum, defined-risk option premium/VRP, futures trend) are all positional; a momentum basket long-only earned +11.8%/yr unhedged vs +4.9% hedged; shorting every 20%-breakdown basket lost ~14%/yr gross — [Tradetron report, Ch. 4–5](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- **Fee model — platforms:** AlgoTest offers free backtesting/paper trading and **paid plans for live deployment** (no published ledger of live results found) — [AlgoTest pricing comparison](https://algotest.in/blog/algo-trading-software-price-in-india/); Streak↔Tradetron and Tradetron↔AlgoTest comparisons describe subscription models across — [Streak vs Tradetron](https://algotest.in/blog/streak-vs-tradetron/), [Tradetron vs AlgoTest](https://tradetron.tech/blog/tradetron-vs-algotest-comparison); Tradetron also runs a marketplace of user strategies with subscriptions/profit-share characteristics (its own active research openly warns its data are "gross, self-selected, not capital-normalized") — [Tradetron research disclaimer](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)
- **smallcase:** fees: subscription for expert-curated smallcases plus a **platform fee ₹10+GST per SIP execution (capped 1.5% of SIP amount)**, and broker transaction fees on top — [smallcase fees page](https://www.smallcase.com/learn/smallcase-fees-and-charges/); [Zerodha value-added services: smallcase ₹100 per one-time buy/invest-more trade, ₹10 per SIP](https://zerodha.com/charges/); separately, expert smallcase subscriptions billed by their RA managers — [smallcase subscription explainer](https://www.smallcase.com/learn/how-does-smallcase-subscription-work/); FY25 monetization: revenue ₹114 cr (52% growth), platform-fee income ₹89.2 cr — [Startup Pedia/LinkedIn citing smallcase FY25 financials](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/startup-pedia_startuppedia-startupjourney-startupbusiness-activity-7447999279769554944-njao); launched US-stock smallcases in 2026 — [ET Markets](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/us-stocks/wall-street-guide/smallcase-launches-us-stock-global-etf-portfolios-for-indian-investors/articleshow/133733016.cms)
- **Broker APIs:** Zerodha Kite Connect ₹500/month ("Connect") — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/); the Feb-2026 regulatory state means retail APIs are legal with static-IP/whitelist/10-OPS constraints — [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations)
- **Regulatory treatment of platform incentives:** SEBI's Feb-2025 circular explicitly permits **subscription and brokerage revenue-sharing between algo providers and stockbrokers** (Zerodha itself called the conflict-of-interest safeguard "self-contradictory" in need of clarification) — [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations); black-box vendors need RA licenses and published strategy reports — [Zerodha explainer](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explaining-the-latest-sebi-algo-trading-regulations)
- **Portfolio-criticism context on survivorship:** general methodology literature documents how "today's survivors' track records" inflate apparent results (0.9–1.4%/yr upward bias in fund universes) — [Elton et al. NYU WP](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/27234/2/wpa94027.pdf); [CFI digest (reverse survivorship)](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/11/reverse-survivorship-bias-digest-summary); India-specific: Tradetron's short-decay universe tests and its disclosure that "today's F&O membership projected backward" *flatters* buy-the-fallen backtests — [Tradetron report, note to Ch.5](https://tradetron.tech/research/state-of-retail-algo-trading-india-2026.html)

### Inferences
- Between Zerodha's ₹100/one-time-invest fee, smallcase's ₹10/SIP + expert subscriptions, ₹500/mo broker API costs, and algo-platform subscriptions, the *platform* layer adds ₹1,200–₹12,000+/yr to a retail quant's cost base — trivial for a positional £60k strategy but often larger than the entire quarterly edge of an options-intraday retail account.
- The combination of (a) legalized broker↔vendor kickbacks, (b) marketplace survivorship (losers delisted, ~2-trade median abandonment), and (c) mostly-backtested presentation means published retail "track records" should be integrally treated as marketing until independently verified. The only substantial *published with-limits live ledger* in India retail quant (Tradetron's) reads as evidence *against* intraday-chasing strategies and *for* positional momentum/VRP/trend premia, consistent with the regulator's churn-friendly but turnover-punished design.
- Corporate reality-check: no retail platform found in 2026 publishes (i) live-vs-model discrepancy, (ii) abandoned-strategy survival curves, or (iii) ROI after costs for its average customer; smallcase monetizes via subscriptions/trade counts rather than performance success, i.e., their incentives align to AUM/churn, not investor alpha.

### Gaps
- No publicly audited live track record for AlgoTest, Streak, QuantMan, or any single smallcase manager was found in accessible pages; smallcase's estimated "model returns" vs real user fills vs delisted smallcases could not be located as published institutional numbers on the site (they list per-plan page but no consolidated "all-managers live salvage" table).
- Tradetron's ledger is the deepest published source but shares deficiencies of any platform-published data: single-platform, self-selected users, gross-of-charges, rupees-not-percent returns, and no external audit.

## Key Question 4: Evidence on retail profitability — SEBI/exchange studies; what the profitable minority have in common

### Takeaway
The latest SEBI study (released 20 Aug 2026, FY25–FY26, top-15-broker data covering ~90% of individual F&O participants) shows **87.7% of individual F&O traders still lost money in FY26** (down from 90.9% in FY25; 93% in FY22–FY24), with **aggregate losses ₹91,685 crore**, ~92% of the losses in options, ~₹25,000 crore/yr consumed by transaction costs, and participation down ~20% YoY; profitability concentrates sharply with larger equity holdings, option *sellers* (the only positive-median-ROCE strategy group), and institutional/prop/FPI accounts — with **99% of FPI and proprietary-trader profits coming from algo entities**. The few retail winners are, per SEBI and Tradetron alike: larger-capital, low-turnover, positional, more often selling (with defined risk) than buying. The loss evidence is derivative-segment specific; no comparable SEBI profitability/churn study exists for cash delivery.

### Inferences
- Cross-referencing SEBI's FY26 study with Tradetron's ledger, the **few consistent retail winners** share: (i) materially larger equity capital relative to trade size (better for capital-resilience and lower relative costs), (ii) **selling or hedged (defined-risk) options** rather than naked buying, (iii) positional/low-turnover horizons (days–months, not day—certainly not 0DTE), and (iv) systematic (algo) execution with cost-awareness — retail losers are the sub-₹1L-portfolio, option-buying, 0DTE-trading, higher-intensity cohort. There is no study providing a margin-carrying daily small account that shows a durable positive net edge ⇒ per the numbers (87.7% losers; ₹1.17L avg loss; 85–90% repeat), the *prior for a new retail daily-directional trader* should be "loss expected after costs unless delivering exceptional capital + patience."
- Delivery (cash equity) has not attracted a SEBI profitability-churn study comparable to derivatives; but the 0.24%+ per round-trip statutory cost (0.2pp of it STT) makes high-frequency delivery algo ideas structurally hard, while weekly–monthly momentum/rebalance cadences (~2.8%–6%/yr of traded value in costs) are arithmetically compatible with the premia Tradetron/academic work says exist (momentum ~11%+/yr, drift). This is the cost-shaped argument for why the *retail-modification* of this entire segment per the FY26 numbers chiefly migrates to positional/momentum only after 2025-26 rule changes.
- Net-net on economics: a well-run, low-turnover, large-cap, long-only delivery algo in India loses ~1/3 to 1/2 pp of its annual gross edge to costs/slippage at a 4–12× annual traded-value turnover — liveable. Anything daily-frequency or 0DTE-shaped loses to the cost meter by default (Tradetron's arithmetic: ₹16k–₹79k/yr of visible+slippage costs per strategy at 1–5 round trips/day, against SEBI's finding that the average losing individual lost ₹1.17 lakh in FY26).
