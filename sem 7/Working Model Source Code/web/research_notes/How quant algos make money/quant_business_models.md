# How Professional Quant Trading Firms Make Money: Business Models, Counterparties, Real Returns, and Infrastructure (state of the world as of November 2026)

Scope note: This is a catalog of dominant profit mechanisms of professional quant firms (hedge funds, market makers, HFT/prop shops, India-specific desks), *who is on the losing side of each mechanism*, what net returns leading firms actually deliver (verified vs. claimed), and the infrastructure each strategy class requires. Figures are dated. India figures are in INR (₹; 1 lakh = 100,000; 1 crore = 10 million; ≈₹8,800 to $1 as of late 2026, figures converted at source rates).

---

## Key Question 1: What are the dominant money-making strategy classes, and where does each one's P&L physically come from (who loses)?

### Takeaway
With the exception of factor premia and long-horizon trend following (which harvest risk premia from other investors' constraints, preferences, and slow reactions), the dominant quant profit mechanisms — high-frequency market making, latency-arbitrage racing, retail order internalization, statistical arbitrage, expiry-day index arbitrage, and volatility selling — take money from identifiable counterparties: un/ill-informed liquidity takers (increasingly retail), passive quote suppliers in speed races, predictably-hedging institutional desks, and in India specifically, a ~90%-loss-rate retail F&O crowd whose flow is directly booked by prop desks and FPIs (96–97% of whose profits are algo-generated per SEBI).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Strategy-by-strategy catalog

1. **High-frequency market making (spread capture at massive turnover).** The business model: quote both sides, earn the bid-ask spread, manage inventory and adverse selection. Academic classification consistently finds most HFT activity is market-making — "widely accepted that the majority of [HFT firms] (about 80%) follow the market-making strategy" (citing Hagströmer & Nordén 2013 and Menkveld 2013) — [Systemic Risk Council DP 113, options-HFT 2020 paper](https://www.systemicrisk.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-01/dp-113.pdf); the empirical literature "overwhelmingly shows that the presence of HFT has increased market quality... by decreasing bid-ask spreads" (Brogaard et al. 2014, Hendershott, Jones & Menkveld 2011, Hasbrouck & Saar 2013, Menkveld 2014) — [EFMA 2020 full paper, Dublin](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2020-Dublin/papers/EFMA%202020_stage-1301_question-Full%20Paper_id-422.pdf). Hendershott, Jones, and Menkveld (2011) showed algorithmic trading "causally improves liquidity and makes quotes more informative" — [Menkveld, "HFT and the New Market Makers" (econstor)](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/86769/1/11-076.pdf). **Who pays the spread:** primarily liquidity-takers executing in the lit market — and crucially, *large institutional investors*: order-level data from four Nordic institutions (APG, DNB, NBIM, Swedbank Robur) shows HFTs first "lean against" big institutional orders (selling into buys, buying into sells) and later "turn around and seem to go with the order" if execution takes hours; the paper argues the debate should shift from bid-ask spreads to end-user transaction cost. The identified HFTs in that study: Citadel, Flow Traders, Getco, IAT, IMC, Knight, Optiver, Spire, Susquehanna, Virtu — [Menkveld blog, "HFT around Large Institutional Orders"](https://albertjmenkveld.com/blog/2015-06-18-high-frequency-trading-around-large-institutional-orders).
   - There is also a **profit hierarchy among different HFT types**: "Aggressive HFTs earn profits from Mixed and Passive HFTs, while Mixed HFTs earn profits from Passive HFTs" — aggressive HFTs made 37.2% of their profits trading against Opportunistic HFTs and 32.8% against Mixed HFTs (E-mini S&P 500 futures data in the authors' sample period); estimated Sharpe ratios were extreme (Mixed HFTs 10.46; Menkveld's studied firm 9.35, per its own earlier paper) — [Baron, Brogaard & Kirilenko, NBER MM conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2012/MMf12/Baron_Brogaard_Kirilenko.pdf).

2. **Latency arbitrage / the speed race.** Using full exchange *message* data (wins and losses visible, not just fills), Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill (FCA/BIS/NBER) find that for FTSE-100 stocks there is ~one latency-arbitrage "race" per symbol per minute, races last 5–10 microseconds, races account for ~20–22% of trading volume, and the top 6 firms win >80% of all races. The "latency arbitrage tax" is ~0.42 bp of trading volume ≈ **£51–63 million per year in UK equities (2014–2017 data)**, extrapolating to **on the order of $5 billion per year across global equity markets**. Races are triggered by symmetric public information (mostly futures-equity and cross-venue index signals, per the Budish-Cramton-Shim 2015 model) — [BIS WP 955](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-955-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race), [NBER w29011](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/29011.html), [Harvard Law forum summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/11/05/quantifying-the-high-frequency-trading-arms-race), [BFI working paper](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/BFI_WP_202086.pdf). **Who loses:** the two losing sides of the tens of thousands of races per day (usually liquidity suppliers whose quotes are picked off, per the Budish-Cramton-Shim framework) — and even aside from the direct transfers, the arms race itself is a socially wasteful competition whose cost lands as an extra "tax" on all trading — [Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill via Harvard Law forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/11/05/quantifying-the-high-frequency-trading-arms-race); in the simulated counterfactual, eliminating latency arbitrage would cut the market's cost of liquidity by 17%, and races constitute ~one-third of price impact/effective spread cost — [NBER w29011](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/29011.html).

3. **Retail order internalization (PFOF wholesaling).** US wholesale market making is a scale business concentrated in a handful of firms: SEC Rule 606 reports show Citadel made 41% of *all* PFOF payments, with Susquehanna and Virtu 2nd/3rd; wholesalers pay brokers on the order of $0.20 per 100 shares for routed equity flow — [Wharton WFFI white paper](https://wffi.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Payment-for-Order-Flow-final.pdf). In *options* the economics are even richer for the wholesaler: "an option trade would give a market maker 100% more revenue than an equity trade," and because the average retail option trade is in a $5 underlying while the average retail stock trade is in a $25 stock, "$1,000 in a $5 option" generates several times the chain revenue of $1,000 in equities — [Wharton WFFI](https://wffi.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Payment-for-Order-Flow-final.pdf). Order-flow concentration in options (Jan 2020–Jun 2021 sample, NBER wp): Citadel 852M option orders/42.2% share, SIG 28.4%, Wolverine 14.3%, Morgan Stanley 7.8%, IMC ("Dash") 6.0%; in stocks Citadel 40.7%, Virtu 30.1%, G1X 12.9%, Two Sigma 9.1% — [Ernst, Lee, Liu & Sanders, NBER w29883](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29883/w29883.pdf). Citadel Securities (self-reported): "#1 destination for U.S. retail order flow, executing more than 35% of total retail market volume," and "largest on-exchange options market maker, with approximately 30% market share across the U.S. listed options markets," with US options ADV >100% higher than 2020 by 2025 — [Citadel Securities](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/what-we-do/options). **Who loses / pays:** under SEC Chair Gensler's framing, >90% of retail market orders are internalized to wholesalers instead of competing on-lit — [SEC public comment record quoting Gensler](https://www.sec.gov/comments/s7-31-22/s73122-327434.htm). Counter-evidence from academic and industry studies: retail gets better average prices than the lit exchange would provide, and Citadel Securities claimed the SEC's 2022 auction proposal "would actually result in retail investors losing $2.4 billion per year" — [Citadel Securities comment letter, Mar 31 2023](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/03/Citadel-Securities-Response-to-the-Auctions-Proposal-Final.pdf); Barron's summarized the academic study finding PFOF "perhaps shouldn't worry the SEC" — [Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/payment-for-order-flow-sec-51661280732). The honest framing: the market maker earns the retail spread plus the informational edge of seeing all retail flow, at a cost advantage vs. exchange-based executions.

4. **Statistical arbitrage / systematic market-neutral equity.** Profits come from short-horizon relative mispricings (pairs, e.g., ETF fairs, sub-industry baskets) that converge; the counterparties include anyone forced to trade the wrong leg at the wrong time, including other algorithms' detectable behavior: liquidity-*demanding* HFTs notoriously "exploit the intraday (temporary) price impact generated by options market-makers' hedging demand" (predictable hedging flow) — [Systemic Risk DP 113](https://www.systemicrisk.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-01/dp-113.pdf) citing Van Kervel & Menkveld 2019. Also cited theoretical harm channel: speculative HFTs "snipe stale quotes of high-frequency market makers," raising liquidity provision costs (Budish et al. 2015; Menkveld & Zoican 2017) — [ECB WP 2290](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2290~b5fec3a181.en.pdf).

5. **Trend following / CTA (systematic macro momentum).** Harvests long-horizon price drift; the classic structural counterparties are slower than trend: hedgers, producers, and late-reacting discretionary capital. Return evidence: trend following "posted positive returns... across a century of data" and concentrates returns in sustained directional moves — its so-called "crisis alpha": estimated **40–50% of managed-futures returns over the last decade came from equity crisis windows** (NewEdge CTA index, Barclay indices backtest) — [IASG](https://www.iasg.com/blog/2019/12/02/in-search-of-crisis-alpha-a-short-guide-to-investing-in-managed-futures); Man Group's momentum-replica ("momCTA") shows trend "performed particularly well in the worst equity and bond market environments" and this pattern holds in the best windows too, as does replication of the BTOP50 — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-equity-and-bond-crisis-alpha); Greyserman & Kaminski's *Trend Following with Managed Futures* formalized "crisis alpha" — [CFA Institute FAJ review](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2015/trend-following-with-managed-futures). Institutional analyses (MEKETA, Fidelity) treat it as a portfolio hedge whose risk-taking is "dominated by beta timing" rather than relative value — [Fidelity institutional](https://institutional.fidelity.com/institutions/insights/topics/investing-ideas/crisis-alpha-with-a-higher-return-potential), [MEKETA](https://meketa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/MEKETA_Trend-Following.pdf).

6. **Volatility desks / options market making and premium selling.** Listed-options market makers earn the bid-ask spread plus inventory/vega-management alpha; option-selling books earn premium and pay out exactly when realized volatility exceeds what was sold. Concrete evidence of the winning flow side in the world's largest retail-options market (India): FY24 participant-level P&L showed **prop desks gross profit ₹33,000 crore and FPIs ₹28,000 crore, while individuals "and others" lost over ₹61,000 crore (gross)**, and "97% of FPI profits and 96% of proprietary profits came from algorithmic trading" (SEBI's participant-P&L study data, tabulated at CernoQuant) — [CernoQuant](https://cernoquant.com/statistics), anchored by [SEBI's Sept 2024 study release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html). In the US the same retail options flow is what makes wholesaling unusually lucrative: "an option trade would give a market maker 100% more revenue than an equity trade," with CNBC coverage of the 2020–21 retail options boom noting "most are playing a losing game" (both quoted in the Wharton report) — [Wharton WFFI](https://wffi.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Payment-for-Order-Flow-final.pdf).

7. **ETF / index arbitrage, basis trading, and expiry-day "gamma vs. delta" books.** Classic cash-futures and ETF-premium/discount arb arbitrages away by design; the profit source is any mis-priced basis from dislocations. India's biggest recent enforcement case (SEBI interim order, July 3 2025) alleges Jane Street generated illegal profits via expiry-day positioning — SEBI computed net profits of **₹4,843 crore (~$565M) on the numbered "manipulation days" out of an estimated ₹32,681 crore (~$4B) total earned in India**, alleging Jane Street "operated through multiple entities to amplify market impact" and that options exposure vs. total delta exposure ran 7.3:1, with cash-market losses offset by options gains; Jane Street's defense is that this was standard index arbitrage, and it notes SEBI's own surveillance had previously passed it — [FTI Consulting analysis](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/when-algorithmic-trading-meets-allegations-market-manipulation), [SEBI interim order, Jul 3 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/enforcement/orders/jul-2025/interim-order-in-the-matter-of-index-manipulation-by-jane-street-group_95040.html); a LinkedIn summary recaps: "ban lifted after Jane Street deposited ₹4,843.5 crore; the case is now before the Securities Appellate Tribunal and SEBI's wider probe continues" — [LinkedIn summary (secondary)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/albin-joseph-a3899b2ab_sebi-janestreet-indianmarkets-activity-7515248531746586624-ibK3). To size the P&L class: Jane Street made "more than $2.3bn in equity-derivatives revenue in India in 2024" (reported trading revenue, not client fees) — [Young & Calculated substack, 2026](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/jane-street-booked-396bn-in-2025). Counterparty context: Bank Nifty is ~50% weighted by just two stocks (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank), which is exactly why large Bank-Nifty-heavy positioning was flagged — [The Stock Learning (blog-level source)](https://www.thestocklearning.co.in/blog-details/inside-sebi’s-probe-into-jane-street’s-trading-tactics-in-india-what-you-should-know).

8. **Factor investing / systematic multi-asset premia.** Not an "edge vs. named counterparty" but a risk-premium harvesting argument: AQR-style style-premia investing and multi-strat funds (AQR Apex $6.8B) rebalance to capture value/momentum/quality/defensive spreads from investors with other constraints, horizon preferences, or flows — AQR Apex returned 19.6% in 2025 [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium). **Event-driven** (special-situations engines such as Kite Lake, +17.9% for 2025) systematically harvest risk premia around offers/liquidations — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium). Both classes feed on other investors' constraints, horizons, and reluctance to capitalize on spreads rather than on a named trading counterparty per trade.

#### B. Historical context on the 2010s HFT "good or bad for markets" debates (now largely settled/relegated)
- The pro-HFT academic side (Hendershott, Jones & Menkveld 2011 via NYSE; Brogaard 2011/2019; Hasbrouck & Saar 2013; Menkveld 2013 on Chi-X): algorithmic trading causally improved liquidity, price efficiency, and reduced spreads — [EFMA 2020 literature review](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2020-Dublin/papers/EFMA%202020_stage-1301_question-Full%20Paper_id-422.pdf), [Menkveld, "HFT & the New Market Makers"](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/86769/1/11-076.pdf).
- The anti-HFT side (Budish-Cramton-Shim 2015; Menkveld & Zoican 2017): the arms race burns social cost fighting for ~$5B/year of pure latency rents; harm to liquidity provision is conditional on "the security news to liquidity trader ratio" (whether fast speculative types or internalizers dominate gains) — [ECB WP 2290](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2290~b5fec3a181.en.pdf).
- Regulatory interest faded with redesigns (frequent-batch auction pilots not implemented broadly) and market-structure changes (SEBIs-class CFM-type market-maker programs); measurement papers (CFTC 2022 over 2012–2021) conclude market-quality effects remain positive overall and link HFT presence to market quality without finding a decline over the decade — [CFTC, "HFT and Market Quality," 2022](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/2022-08/HFT_and_market_quality_ada.pdf).

### Inferences
- If one maps each strategy to its funding counterparty, the general pattern is: **HFT market making and internalization harvest uninformed retail/institutional flow; stat-arb harvests predictable mechanical flow (hedges, index reconstitutions, other detectable institutional patterns); trend following harvests large slow-moving directional capital and hedging demand; vol-selling desks harvest impatient option buyers (retail lottery demand); expiry-day index arb in India harvests expiry-crowd payoffs (which SEBI claims crossed into manipulation).**
- The $5B/yr global equity latency-arb estimate being small relative to $12–40B firm revenues (Jane Street, Citadel Securities) implies most HFT revenue is *not* pure latency arb but spread capture, PFOF economics, and cross-market basis trading (which inflow-based counterparty sources confirm).
- The very high realized Sharpe ratios in Baron et al.'s CME data (9–10) versus sub-2.0 Sharpe ratios in discretionary multi-strat funds implies frequency of trading opportunities is the single biggest structural differentiator of the "alpha density" possible per unit risk.

### Gaps
- No single public source aggregates "who exactly loses money to statistical arbitrage firms" with an audited attribution; the academic evidence is via microstructure mechanisms (stale quote sniping, predictable hedging flow), not P&L accounting.
- Trend-following "who loses" is qualitative — the specific counterparties in crisis alpha (hedgers, late-reacting investors) are inferred, not directly measured in a primary paper within this research set.
- Full €/$-denominated 2025 revenue for Flow Traders, Hudson River Trading, IMC and others was not fully collected in this pass; only representative firm-level revenues (Jane Street, Citadel Securities, XTX, Optiver) are included.

## Key Question 2: Net annual returns leading quant firms actually deliver; verified vs. claimed

### Takeaway
The single highest-return quant fund (Renaissance Medallion) has a ~39–40% *net* CAGR since 1988 (66% gross) built on a $10–15B AUM cap and employee-only access, per author-reported/leaked numbers rather than audited public statements; the major investor-facing quant multi-strats (D.E. Shaw, AQR, Citadel, Millennium) deliver far more modest mid-teens returns (2025: AQR Apex 19.6%, D.E. Shaw Composite 18.5%, Citadel Wellington 10.2%) that are "verified" only via press sourcing ("a person close to the firm"), while market makers' P&L is genuinely verifiable firm revenue (Jane Street's $39.6B in 2025 — the highest single firm-year trading result on record).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Renaissance / Medallion — the gold standard, but not publicly audited
- Net return average **~39–40% annually since 1988** (66% gross before fees of 5% management + 44% incentive); total trading profits **>$100 billion**; only firms like Buffett/Soros/Lynch/Dalio "fall short" — quote from Zuckerman's *The Man Who Solved the Market* — [Wikipedia, Renaissance Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies); sourced from the book, i.e., author-reported, not SEC filings. Medallion's aggregate structure has been **closed to outside investors since 1993** (employees only) and is **capped around $10–15B AUM**, distributing excess profits annually (therefore the CAGR is "highly theoretical") — [Wikipedia RenTec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies), [Quartr, "Renaissance Technologies and the Medallion Fund"](https://quartr.com/insights/edge/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund).

#### D. How the "verified vs. claimed" breakdown actually works (methodological note)
- Hedge fund return figures are fundamentally **claims via journalist sourcing** ("a person close to the firm said...") — see sourcing in [Business Insider Jan 2026](https://www.businessinsider.com/2025-hedge-funds-returns-performance-balyasny-millennium-exoduspoint-2026-1) and [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium). No hedge fund discloses audited returns publicly anymore; databases like BarclayHedge/HFR rely on voluntary reporting.
- Market-maker revenues are **spotted via two channels**: (a) lender disclosures — Jane Street/Citadel Securities report semiannually to financing banks (Bloomberg/Reuters' reporting), and (b) statutory/Companies House filings (XTX), Dutch statutory filings (Optiver), Indian MCA statutory filings (India entities) — the most "verifiable" numbers.
- Medallion's numbers are, strictly speaking, a **category of their own**: author/insider-sourced figures compiled in Zuckerman's 2019 book and subsequent reporting; they are neither SEC filings nor audited statements, but they are stable across independent sources (30% net for 2024; ~39% net / ~66% gross average since 1988) — [Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/greatest-fund-ever-why-jim-142555191.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies), [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/edge/renaissance-technologies-and-the-medallion-fund).

### Inferences
- The gap between Medallion and the investable quant multi-strats — Medallion ~40% net/yr for three decades vs. 10–20% for AQR/D.E. Shaw/Citadel/Millennium — shows that publicly visible mid-teens returns, not 40%, are the realistic ceiling for investor-scale products: Medallion's structural inputs (AUM hard-capped ~$10–15B, employee-only capital since 1993, a ~275-person institution of mathematicians/physicists/engineers) cannot be replicated at institutional scale.
- The consistent **midpoint pattern** — 16–20% claimed returns for AQR/D.E. Shaw/Point72/ExodusPoint in 2025 against the S&P 500's 16.4% total return — indicates that mid-teens is the credible top band for diversified quant multi-strategy products at investor scale, with the caveat that every one of those figures is claimed (news-sourced), not audited.
- The **market-maker revenue surge of 2025–Q1 2026 (Jane Street $39.6B, then $16.1B Q1 2026; Citadel Securities $12.2B)** indicates extraordinarily high trading volume/volatility and that the total revenue pool available to top-tier professional firms is roughly $50B+/yr in the US alone, versus ~$10–20B levels two to three years earlier.

### Gaps
- **No verified 2025 (or YTD 2026) Medallion return was available** — the Medallion fund does not report publicly; any "2025 Medallion return" you find is a follower-estimate and should be treated skeptically.
- One garbled lead on AQR's 2025 public funds: an eFinancialCareers pay article mentions that AQR's public funds "posted 15% losses" at some point in 2025 and "bounced back with double-digit returns in November for returns of ~2% each" — the context was unclear/possibly garbled in that source, so treat as unverified noise — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/biggest-quant-hedge-funds).
- No public per-strategy breakdown of 2025 returns (i.e., we cannot tell whether a Composite fund's +18.5% came from arb vs. discretionary book), so cross-firm strategy-level comparisons carry high uncertainty.
- Two Sigma 2026 YTD not found; 2024 Two Sigma full-year net return not found either (only April-2024 partial metrics and a 14.3% aggregator figure).

## Key Question 3: India specifically — SEBI prop regime, colocation, F&O-heavy books, named firms, returns, and what share of NSE is algo/prop

### Takeaway
India's quant complex is dominated by F&O (options) arbitrage/market-making on NSE: **~59–60% of NSE index/stock-derivatives turnover and ~29% of cash turnover is proprietary trading** (SEBI/NSE participant data FY24–FY25), and the algo share across segments rose through FY26 (cash ~54%, equity derivatives ~67%, stock futures 73%), with SEBI's own studies showing **93% of ~1.13 crore retail F&O traders lost a combined ₹1.81 lakh crore FY22–FY24, with 95–97% of prop/FPI profits algo-generated — i.e., Indian quant trading's counterparty is overwhelmingly retail** — and the specific players (Graviton, AlphaGrep, Quadeye, iRage, HRT India, Optiver India, Jane Street, Citadel Securities India, Estee) are growing their India books despite SEBI's Feb 2025 retail-algo framework, Oct 2024 index-derivatives tightening, and the July 2025 Jane Street ban case.

### Cited Findings

#### A. What share of NSE turnover is prop / algo (primary data)
- **Prop vs. other (SEBI "Current Statistics," June 2024 annexure, using NSE participant-category data):** Cash segment turnover FY2023-24: Proprietary 28.2%, FPIs 14.4%, MFs 7.8%, Others 49.3%; FY2024-25: **Proprietary 29.2%, FPIs 15.0%, MFs 8.5%**. Equity derivatives segment: **Prop (F&O turnover share) 59.7% FY2023-24 and 59.0% FY2024-25**, FPIs 6.0%/7.3%, Others 34.3%/33.6%; in *open interest* terms, props are only 22.7%/20.5% (i.e., prop turnover/OI skew = they churn volume, don't carry big books). Index derivatives by instrument FY2024-25: **BankNifty 40.2% of turnover, Nifty 37.6%, FinNifty 15.8%** — [SEBI Current Statistics Annexure (Aug 2024, later-updated tables)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/aug-2024/Annexure_Tables_revised_June_2024_p.pdf).
- **Algo share by segment (NSE TBT/MTBT-based, as tabulated by QuantInsti, FY15→FY26TD):** Stock futures algo participation 39%→**73%**; Equity futures 39%→**69%**; Overall equity derivatives 50%→**67%**; Options (equity) 56%→~60% (peaking 64% in FY20); Cash market 39%→**54%** (steady growth); Index futures 39%→**53%** — [QuantInsti, "Algorithmic Trading in India (2026)"](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algorithmic-trading-india). So the honest summary: **prop is ~30% of cash turnover and ~59% of F&O turnover; algo is roughly half of cash turnover and ~two-thirds of derivatives** — overlapping but distinct categories (prop includes discretionary too; algo share includes non-prop FPI/client-code algos).
- The Indian F&O complex is arguably the largest in the world by contract volume and is heavily driven by retail option-buyer flow; the specific flow: "index options turnover is ~59% on expiry day... ~97% of index options turnover comes from within a week of expiry" (per the FY26 study summary clip) — [Moneycontrol-style study clip on YouTube, Aug 2026 study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_M2LjBxJugY) and the earlier [Moneylife, covering the FY24-25 study](https://www.moneylife.in/article/91-percentage-of-retail-traders-lost-money-in-derivatives-losses-in-fo-surged-41-percentage-to-rs105-lakh-crore-in-fy2425-sebi-study/77613.html).

#### B. Who pays Indian quant P&L (SEBI's own studies, primary)
- **SEBI study, Sept 2024 (FY22–FY24): 93% of 1.13 crore (≈11.3m) individual F&O traders lost money; aggregate losses >₹1.81 lakh crore (~$22B); average loss ~₹2 lakh per event trader; FY24 alone: top 3.5% of loss-makers averaged ₹28 lakh loss each; transaction costs consumed 28% of total net losses** — [SEBI press release (primary)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html), [Saral (concise tabulation)](https://saral.money/blog/why-93-percent-of-fo-traders-lose).
- **SEBI follow-up study (published ~Aug 2025 on FY25 and Dec-2024–May-2025 post-reform window): 91% of individual F&O traders in FY25 lost money; net losses up 41% YoY to ₹1,05,603 crore (from ₹74,812 crore in FY24); average per-person loss ₹1.1 lakh; unique traders fell from 61.4 lakh (Q1 FY25) to 42.7 lakh (Q4)** after SEBI's expiry-day/lot-size/margin reform package — [Moneylife (coverage)](https://www.moneylife.in/article/91-percentage-of-retail-traders-lost-money-in-derivatives-losses-in-fo-surged-41-percentage-to-rs105-lakh-crore-in-fy2425-sebi-study/77613.html); SEBI later **revised the FY25 figure to ₹1.12 lakh crore in its Aug 2026 study** — [CernoQuant tabulation](https://cernoquant.com/statistics).
- **FY26 study (Aug 2026): ~88% of individual F&O traders lost money (87.7%); net losses 72,243 crore; participation down ~20% YoY; 59% of index options turnover on expiry day; 95% of 5-year-sustained participants still lose money; per-person average loss ~₹1.1 lakh** — [YouTube study-summary clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_M2LjBxJugY) and [CernoQuant](https://cernoquant.com/statistics). Cumulative FY22–FY26 net retail losses ≈ **₹3.6 lakh crore (about $41B)** — computed from SEBI figures as aggregated at [CernoQuant](https://cernoquant.com/statistics).

This is the cleanest public "who pays whom" stat in Indian quant trading: **algorithmic prop + FPI flow takes in nearly all the aggregate retail F&O bleed.**

#### C. Regulatory architecture: prop regime, algo framework, colocation
- **SEBI's Feb 4 2025 circular** "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading" (SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000013) + NSE implementation circular (NSE/INVG/67858, May 5 2025): retail algo trading must route through registered brokers with exchange-registered algos and exchange-assigned Algo IDs; algo *providers* must empanel with exchanges; execution algos get a "fast-tag" registration track — [NSE algo trading page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-non-neat-decision-support-tools-algorithm-trading), [NSE Open House video summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A-bsHzcYBhY), [Legal500 analysis](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/sebi's-new-algo-trading-regulations-a-game-changer-for-retail-investors). Position limits, expiry-day and contract lot-size reforms from Oct 1 2024 reshaped derivatives risk — see [QuantPower SEBI/NSE rules summary (2026)](https://quantpower.tech/sebi-rules-algo-trading), also referencing the Jan-2026 revision of index-derivative market lots.
- **Prop trading under SEBI:** Indian "prop" desks are typically *exchange trading members' proprietary books* (as seen in the SEBI participant tables above) rather than SEBI-registered AIF funds; the prop/FPI/individual/MF categorization in the statistical annexure is the canonical segmentation — [SEBI Current Statistics](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/aug-2024/Annexure_Tables_revised_June_2024_p.pdf).
- **Colocation:** NSE offers rack-based colocation in Mumbai: **half rack ₹4.5 lakh one-time + ₹50,000/month recurring, with a 40-unit throughput allocation and 1 default + 1 additional on-demand port pair; quarter rack ₹2.25 lakh one-time + ₹25,000/month, 20-unit throughput, 1 default port pair; 10 Gbps order and market-data connectivity; additional connectivity pair ₹75,000/year; backup-restore facility ₹66,000/rack** (the full-rack tier is proportionally double the half-rack footprint/fees) — [NSE co-location facility page (current price schedule)](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-co-location-facility). NSE's published reference latency (rack ↔ core router, no-load) is **~18.9 microseconds**, and NSE has expanded colocation capacity to **1,200+ racks** (Jan 2025, per Reuters) — [Endovia Wealth explainer citing NSE circulars (Apr–Jun 2025 reference data)](https://www.endoviawealth.com/how-latency-affects-trade-execution-lessons-from-the-pros). Also relevant: "Colocation as a Service" (CAAS) offered via vendors like Symphony FinTech with TBT feed handlers and 100-depth subscriptions — [Symphony FinTech](https://symphonyfintech.com/caas-colocation-as-a-service).
- Trading-through-facilities: latency-sensitive prop firms historically get exchange access via what are called "broker DMA/multisegment memberships" (a niche service-provider layer exists: e.g., **Estee Advisors** — SEBI-registered broker across NSE/BSE/MCX/MSEI/GIFT, which since 2008 provides "an end-to-end solution... both the software suite (normalised API and RMS platforms) and the co-located infrastructure (high-performance servers, leased lines)" for HFTs and latency-sensitive traders, including at GIFT City — i.e., India has an entire *infrastructure-rental* slice of the quant ecosystem — [Estee Advisors](https://esteeadvisors.com), [Estee Advisors LinkedIn description](https://www.linkedin.com/company/estee-advisors).

### Inferences
- **Indian quant P&L is structurally retail-funded** in a way that has no true US analogue: the 88–93% loss-rate retail F&O pool losing ₹1–2 lakh crore/yr net *is* the "spread-plus-tail" income for a concentrated set of prop desks and FPIs whose profits are 96–97% algo-generated (SEBI's own data). This also implies Indian prop-desk revenue tracks growth in retail participant counts and expiry-day flow rather than index levels.
- **The prop/F&O concentration of Graviton, HRT India, Optiver and AlphaGrep** in the 2024–2026 era tracks the index-expiry crowding (BankNifty 40% / Nifty 38% of index-derivatives turnover, per the SEBI annexure) and the retail-flow boom of 2020–2024.
- **The regulatory floor shifted hard in 2024–2025:** Oct 2024 index-derivatives tightening (lot size, expiry consolidation, position limits) cut retail flows visibly (61.4→42.7 lakh unique traders through FY25), and Feb 2025 retail-algo framework re-legalizes-but-rails retail algo flow through registered brokers/exchange-registered algos (algo ID per order, algo-provider empanelment) — both changes *re-route* rather than *eliminate* the retail F&O flow, so continued prop-desk profit growth onto FY26 (+156%, +77%, +17%) is a natural output.
- **The market-making infrastructure in India is materially different from the US:** NSE colo ~18.9μs rack-reference latency at modest absolute cost (₹4.5 lakh one-time + ~₹50k/month per half-rack + connectivity rents) is orders of magnitude cheaper than US-tier low-latency infrastructure — a real opportunity floor for a well-funded individual or small team, with the caveat that colo message-rate allocations and, above all, racing against incumbents' incumbent infrastructure govern HFT competitiveness.

### Gaps
- **Graviton's ₹-denominated profit figure ("nearly 12 billion rupees," +17%) is Bloomberg-sourced with an ambiguous fiscal-year label** ("fiscal year that ended on March 31" — which March is not stated). Given Indian company-filing cycles and the article's Oct 2026 publication, it is most likely FY2026 (ended Mar 2026) or FY2025 (ended Mar 2025); reported here as stated without picking one.
- **No Indian quant/prop firm has ever published an investor "return" figure** — annual profit numbers from MCA/regulatory filings are revenue/profit levels, not returns on capital; computing a meaningful return would require equity-base data not surfaced in this research.
- **"i-Nav" branded desk:** no evidence of a specifically branded "i-NAV desk" in Indian quant trading surfaced; iNAV-based ETF arbitrage is a strategy class, not a firm-specific product.
- **Post-Feb-2025 census of algo share by segment (cash/derivatives, FY26)**: no post-circular quantitative breakdown was found in the sources reached; the FY26TD algo shares (cash 54%, derivatives 67%) are trailing figures via QuantInsti's NSE-based tabulation.

## Key Question 4: Infrastructure each class requires — what an individual can replicate vs. institutionally gated

### Takeaway
There is a four-way stratification: (1) market making / latency-arb infrastructure is gated by exchange-message entitlements, colocation physicality, and C++/FPGA stacks (vendor-modeled setup of $200k+ upfront and tens-of-thousands/month running cost), (2) statistical arbitrage / multi-strategy is gated by *data* (multi-terabyte tick history across venues; professional market-data feeds run roughly $10k–50k/month per venue class on the vendor's tier table) and *compute* (XTX runs 25,000 GPUs — i.e., compute is a binding constraint), (3) trend/CTA factor and event-driven strategies are gated mainly by capital scale and risk management institutionally, but are technically replicable by an individual at small size — and (4) the individual-accessible floor is far higher (cheaper) in India, where NSE colocation (₹4.5 lakh one-time + ₹50k/month) is dramatically below US-tier low-latency costs. The dominant edge is thus rarely "code" alone; it is entitlements, data, and message-rate permissions.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Speed/market-making infrastructure (what HFT firms consume)
- **Exchange-side gating: message-level speed, not quotes, decides latency arb.** The highest-value microstructure information is in exchange *messages* (attempted-but-failed orders visible only in raw feeds); competing for 5–10μs race windows requires direct exchange/gateway access, full message-feed entitlements, and race-winning infrastructure (colo racks, cross-connects, kernel bypass/FPGA stacks) — race wins are concentrated: top 6 firms took >80% of all race wins and losses in UK data — [BIS WP 955 (Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill)](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-955-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race).
- **US HFT infrastructure cost estimates (customer-side, from a trading-vendor model):** "ultra-low-latency" classification (500ns–1ms equities) runs **$5,000–15,000/month for colo+power, $3,000–10,000/month for cross-connects, $10,000–50,000/month for market data feeds, $5,000–20,000/month broker/venue memberships**; their summary table: "If your edge lives milliseconds — colocation with a bare metal server, a Solarflare/Mellanox card, kernel bypass, and good C++ code will cost you $3,000–5,000 per month... If you need sub-500 microseconds — prepare $200,000–400,000 upfront and $36,000–72,000 per month" — [WesternPips cost model (vendor estimate, use directionally)](https://westernpips.com/hft-infrastructure-cost.html).
- **India colocation pricing (primary):** half-rack **one-time ₹4.5 lakh + ₹50,000/month recurring**, quarter-rack ₹2.25L + ₹25k/month; 10 Gbps order and market-data ports; an additional connectivity pair is ₹75,000/year — [NSE co-location](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-co-location-facility); NSE's reference rack-to-core-router latency ~18.9μs, 1,200+ racks — [Endovia Wealth (citing NSE/Reuters)](https://www.endoviawealth.com/how-latency-affects-trade-execution-lessons-from-the-pros). For participants that cannot take colo racks, the next-best options are leased-line P2P to the broker, dual market-data channels with redundancy, and rigorous pre-trade risk checks, per NSE's own guidance — [Endovia Wealth](https://www.endoviawealth.com/how-latency-affects-trade-execution-lessons-from-the-pros).
- **What cannot work from outside the colo:** latency-arbitrage strategies executed from remote/home-hosted servers — modal race windows are 5–10μs, reachable only inside the exchange fabric; even low-millisecond VPS paths are orders of magnitude too slow to ever win a race (race length and 18.9μs reference latency figures above).

#### B. Data / compute / research infrastructure (stat-arb, factor, multi-strat)
- **XTX Markets as a microbenchmark of modern quant infrastructure:** ~250 staff, ~$250B daily notional traded, **25,000 GPUs in the research cluster (2025)**, and coverage of 50,000+ instruments across equities, FI, FX, commodities and crypto (i.e., a GPU cluster the size of a mid-sized AI lab serving one trading firm) — [Jawlah on 2025 filings](https://jawlah.co/en/63361), [LinkedIn summary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/luca-lacharlotte_xtx-markets-generated-393-billion-in-revenue-activity-7446120611682144257-yr90).
- **Two Sigma's scale:** ~2,410 headcount with ~1,423 in North America — [Tavily company-knowledge page (LinkedIn-sourced, treat as estimate)](https://tavily.com/knowledge/company/9teBHlU8sPV6) — indicating the research-compute and data-warehouse staff footprint of a US multi-strat quant.
- **Revenue-per-employee benchmark (Jane Street):** $6.93M revenue per employee in 2024 on 2,960 staff — i.e., top market makers amortize large infrastructure/technology investment into extraordinary output per head — [Navnoor Bawa research](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/market-making-in-derivatives-crushes).
- **Medallion-style research infrastructure:** Renaissance employs ~275 staff (about 100 of them "qualified purchasers"), and Medallion averaged a reported 71.8% annual return (gross) from 1994 through mid-2014 — [Wikipedia Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies). The deliverable of this class of firm is a large specialist *research* team, not exchange speed.
- **India's broker-mediated rail:** formal algo trading requires **registered brokers, exchange-empanelled algo providers, exchange-assigned algo IDs, and RMS/threshold (pre-trade risk) checks**, so even small Indian quant systems are broker-mediated — [NSE algo-trading page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-non-neat-decision-support-tools-algorithm-trading); and even "high-frequency" access in India can be rented through infrastructure service providers like Estee (broker + co-located infra + API stack) — [Estee Advisors](https://esteeadvisors.com).

#### C. What an individual/fund-of-one *can replicate* vs. what is institutionally gated
- **Can replicate (commodity, non-gated):** signal research and execution on second/min-level data sourced from paid APIs; long-horizon factor tilts approachable via retail-accessible public AQR funds/ETFs (e.g., funds/disclosure-heavy share classes — AQR markets public mutual funds and multiple styles for retail accessibility) — [AQR funds portal](https://funds.aqr.com), [Nobilior aggregator](https://nobilior.com/blog/2025/09/05/best-quant-funds-to-watch-in-202-performance-and-analysis); cross-asset momentum/trend logic that has been publicly replicated out-of-sample (Man Group's "momCTA" replication tracks the BTOP50 managed-futures index) — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-equity-and-bond-crisis-alpha); ETF/iNAV arbitrage in India where exchange-published iNAV updates roughly every 15 seconds for equity ETFs — [Paytm Money](https://www.paytmmoney.com/blog/etf-pricing-nav-inav-fair-value-explained).
- **Cannot replicate without institutional infrastructure:** (a) exchange message data + microsecond-tier, colo-built market making (Budish's races depend on the fastest 5–10μs strategies; the top-6 firms win >80% of wins/losses — [BIS/Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-955-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race)); (b) retail-flow internalization requires a broker-of-record arrangement, PFOF arrangements, and hundreds-of-millions-scale retail order flow pools (Citadel 41% of PFOF payments; 42% of US options internalization) — [NBER w29883](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29883/w29883.pdf), [Wharton WFFI](https://wffi.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Payment-for-Order-Flow-final.pdf); (c) full-depth multi-day liquid F&O/ETF/cash-futures **bid-ask and expiry-rate books at Indian prop-desk size**, which require bank/capital facilities and MCA-registered entities; (d) GPU scale-ML research at 25,000-GPU class, multi-terabyte indices — [Jawlah](https://jawlah.co/en/63361).
- **Where India specifically differs from the US for individuals:** colo access is comparatively cheap and broker-mediated; infrastructure service providers (e.g., Estee) resell ultra-low-latency stacks to latency-sensitive clients *for a fee* (order of ₹2.25–4.5 lakh one-time for rack access plus ₹25–50k/month plus connectivity/data fees, per NSE's published rate card) — [Estee Advisors](https://esteeadvisors.com), [NSE co-location](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-co-location-facility). This lowers the minimum ticket size of serious Indian HFT but imposes the operational requirement that every order runs through the broker's exchange-registered algo-ID framework — [NSE algo-trading page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-non-neat-decision-support-tools-algorithm-trading).

### Inferences
- **The single most robust "gating factor" is the exchange-data/subscription regime, not money, code or compute.** Because latency races and message-level competition decide the *entitlement* to HFT profit, a one-million-dollar individual with an ideal broker/hosting stack can earn a fair absolute return but cannot compete on sub-millisecond speed or full-depth books; the realistic individually-accessible strategy classes are (a) long-horizon factor tilts (via retail-accessible funds/ETFs), (b) trend/momentum across diverse asset universes, (c) mid-frequency statistical arbitrage on liquid large-caps, and (d) ETF/iNAV and cash-futures basis arbitrage.
- **India's F&O-heavy market rewards *a specific type* of small-capital pool with no US analogue:** the retail option-buyer pool at expiry (SEBI: ~59% of index options turnover on expiry day; 88–93% of "individual traders" losing ₹1–2 lakh crore/yr net), plus the regulated ~15-second iNAV dissemination regime that makes ETF-arb feasible at modest ticket sizes — [SEBI via Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/91-percentage-of-retail-traders-lost-money-in-derivatives-losses-in-fo-surged-41-percentage-to-rs105-lakh-crore-in-fy2425-sebi-study/77613.html), [CernoQuant](https://cernoquant.com/statistics), [YouTube study-clip summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_M2LjBxJugY), [Paytm Money](https://www.paytmmoney.com/blog/etf-pricing-nav-inav-fair-value-explained).
- **The infrastructure input mix is being recomposed:** even "speed" firms increasingly must compete on *models* — as XTX's 2025 25,000-GPU/LM-class investment, Optiver's own 2025 annual report, or push on AI-driven trading and systematic research into new asset classes shows, capital-allocation into compute rather than into latencies now carries higher marginal return — [Optiver (primary)](https://www.optiver.com/insights/news/optiver-reports-robust-financial-results-for-2025), [Jawlah XTX](https://jawlah.co/en/63361), [Rupak Ghose](https://rupakghose.substack.com/p/traders-of-amsterdam-part-ii).

### Gaps
- **No primary source provides line-item infrastructure cost structures for Medallion/D.E. Shaw/Two Sigma-class** (research compute budgets, data-warehouse spend, team×-PhD cost structures); only firm-level headcounts and revenue are public.
- **No public data on "individual-with-quant-stack realized returns"** — i.e., whether a well-funded individual quant can systematically achieve 20–40%/yr is not measurable from public sources; the only direct evidence is that the retail F&O pool loses money 88–93% of traders per year in every SEBI study to date.
- **Missing 2025/2026 expense-side figures for Indian colocation latency data** — while NSE's 18.9μs reference is from Apr–Jun 2025, the implied cost of a competitive message-rate upgrade path was not available from source.
