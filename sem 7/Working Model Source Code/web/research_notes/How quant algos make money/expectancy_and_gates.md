# Expectancy Math & Strategy Gating: When a Directional Forecast Stream Becomes Net-of-Cost Profitable — and How Firms Decide It Isn't Luck

Scope: exact math of converting a directional hit-rate stream into per-trade expectancy net of round-trip cost (~0.4% assumed, justified against actual Indian delivery costs below); sample sizes needed to detect an edge at t≈2 / DSR-significance; empirical magnitudes of 5-session cross-sectional excess returns on an NIFTY-100-style universe; and the professional gating ladder (walk-forward/OOS → paper → small live → scale, with deflated-Sharpe/PBO style tests, preregistration, and alpha-decay adjustments). All arithmetic below uses per-trade units in % of position value; "s" = per-trade return standard deviation, "e" = per-trade (net) expectancy, "c" = round-trip cost, "p" = hit rate, "W"/"L" = average win/loss magnitudes.

## Key Question 1: Expectancy math — what hit rate / win-loss asymmetry clears a 0.4% round-trip hurdle on 5-20-session index-relative picks?

### Takeaway
With payoffs that look like slightly-truncated normals (avg win ≈ avg loss ≈ 0.8 × per-trade σ ≈ ±4% on ~4% pick vol), a 0.4% round-trip cost pushes the breakeven hit rate to ~55% — the breakeven IC is ≈ 0.07-0.10 on a 4-6% pick, above the IC good real-world stock-selection signals typically deliver (~0.02-0.05). A slightly-better-than-random ranker on near-normal 5-day residual returns shows hit rate ~51-55% and payoff ratio ~1.0-1.05, so "asymmetry" does not rescue profitability: the cost hurdle is cleared either by genuinely larger hit rate/IC, by deliberate stop/target geometry (which buys payoff ratio at the price of hit rate), or by trading cheaper.

### Formulas (each with source)
- Net per-trade expectancy: `E = p·W − (1−p)·L − c` where W, L are net average win/loss magnitudes. With no breakeven trades and before separate cost: `E = p·W − (1−p)·L` — [TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/trading-expectancy), [QuantStock calculator](https://quantstock.cc/calculators/required-win-rate), [SpreadSheetsHub](https://spreadsheetshub.com/blogs/articles/trading-metrics-that-matter-expectancy), [PineConnector docs](https://docs.pineconnector.com/metrics)
- Breakeven gross win rate: `p* = L / (W + L) = 1 / (1 + payoff ratio)`; with a constant per-trade cost c subtracted from every trade, the solution becomes `p* = (L + c) / (W + L)` — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/risk-reward-ratio-trading-expectancy-relationship), [Traders Second Brain](https://traderssecondbrain.com/guides/expectancy-formula), [SpreadSheetsHub table: payoff 1.0 → 50% breakeven, 1.5 → 40%, 2.0 → 33.3%](https://spreadsheetshub.com/blogs/articles/trading-metrics-that-matter-expectancy), [PineConnector table](https://docs.pineconnector.com/metrics)
- Equivalent normal frame: if a pick's 5-session index-relative return is approximately normal with mean e₀ and std s, then hit rate `p = Φ(e₀/s)` and the implied conditional win/loss magnitudes are `W = s·φ(z)/(1−Φ(z)) + e₀` and `−L = s·φ(z)/Φ(z) − e₀` with `z = e₀/s` (standard truncated-normal moments, arithmetic identity used in the worked examples).
- Alpha-write-down of skill (Grinold & Kahn framework): expected active return proxies `E(R_A) = IC·√BR·σ_A` (fundamental law), i.e. per-name expected excess return scales linearly with IC times the name's volatility — [AnalystPrep (IR* = IC × √BR, E(R_A)* = IC·√BR·σ_A)](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/state-and-interpret-the-fundamental-law-of-active-portfolio-management-including-its-component-terms-transfer-coefficient-information-coefficient-breadth-and-active-risk-aggressiveness), [Financial Edge Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/portfolio-management/fundamental-law-of-active-management), [CFA Institute technical appendix (full law with transfer coefficient)](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/support/programs/cfa/technical-append-to-analysis-of-active-portfolio-mgmt.pdf). Take the per-name corollary `e₀ ≈ IC × σ_pick` (Grinold & Kahn, *Active Portfolio Management*; book itself behind paywall — the same relationship is operationalized in the law cited above).
- Round-trip cost reality check (Indian equity delivery, W.E.F. FY2026-27 rates): STT 0.1% on **both** buy and sell legs — [Zerodha STT explainer](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated), [NSE first-time investor levies page (0.100% each side)](https://www.nseindia.com/static/invest/first-time-investor-sebi-turnover-fees-stt-other-levies);
plus brokerage (0% discount, ~0.1-0.5% full-service), stamp duty 0.015% buy-side, exchange transaction charges ~0.00325%, SEBI fee, 18% GST on services, and ₹20+GST DP charge on sell — [Angel One charge list](https://www.angelone.in/smart-money/trading-courses/list-of-all-trading-fees-and-charges), [SMC trading-charges summary](https://www.smctradeonline.com/blog/trading-account/trading-charges-in-indian-stock-market). Net: ~0.25-0.30% delivery round trip at a discount broker before impact cost; ~0.4% is a fair institutional/impact-inclusive planning number, and NSE's own working paper treats impact cost as a first-order cost item (₹1-lakh sample: explicit costs ~33bps of traded value incl. ~6bps impact; multiples of that on larger/lower-liquidity orders) — [NSE Working Paper, "Cost of Trading in Stock Exchanges"](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSEWP_2.pdf). STT is materially the dominant friction (≈55% of non-brokerage explicit cost) and volume-sensitive — [ICRIER Working Paper 273](https://icrier.org/pdf/working_paper_273.pdf); Zerodha's CEO notes STT collected exceeds brokerage earned for Indian traders — [Nithin Kamath, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nithin-kamath-81136242_securities-transaction-tax-stt-is-the-biggest-activity-7359153100898582528-oFd7).

### Cited findings — the empirical distribution of 5-session cross-sectional (index-relative) returns
- Individual-stock idiosyncratic volatility is large across markets: published cross-sectional estimates imply annualized average idio vol of roughly 50-90% for the extreme months studied (monthly idio-vol estimates of 0.15-0.25 for the January/October samples) — [Iowa State working paper on idiosyncratic-vol premium](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e029f017-6058-41c5-b22b-d62984100945/content); the high-idio-vol quintile in the canonical Ang-Hodrick-Xing-Zhang result is even higher — [Ang et al. "High Idiosyncratic Volatility and Low Returns", Columbia Business School PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3361/ang_high_idiosyncratic_volatility.pdf). A moderate large-cap calibration of ~15-35% annualized idio vol is consistent with these papers' ranges.
- Cross-sectional dispersion of returns (the raw material a ranker slices): the classic cross-country figure is ~4.5% per month (~15.6% annualized) across national markets — [Solnik, "Dispersion as Cross-Sectional Correlation", HKUST-hosted PDF](http://solnik.people.ust.hk/Articles/A7-disper.pdf); within-market stock-level dispersion is conventionally defined as cross-sectional std or mean absolute deviation `CSAD` — [FRBSF working paper on asymmetric cross-sectional dispersion](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp00-18bk.pdf), [EFMA 2017 paper (CSAD definition and quintile stats: low-beta quintile mean monthly return 1.37%, time-series std 7.39%)](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2017-Athens/papers/EFMA2017_0246_fullpaper.pdf), [MSCI quick-take on CSV as dispersion measure (Nov 2023)](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/is-dispersion-in-small-caps-linked-to-selection-opportunities).
- India market context: Nifty 500 realized/defence-sector comparison articles put single-sector index vol in the ~30%+ annualized band — [Motilal Oswal MF, "Nifty 500 Volatility Analysis" (data through June 2025)](https://www.motilaloswalmf.com/motilal-oswal-edge/articles/nifty-500-volatility-analysis); India VIX and Nifty realized vol series are published continuously by NSE — [NSE India VIX historical page](https://www.nseindia.com/reports-indices-historical-vix); long-run Nifty 50 TRI ~10-15%/yr with single-year swings from −51% (2008) to +78% (2009) — [Bajaj AMC Nifty 50 historical returns table (2026 YTD −8.1% included)](https://www.bajajamc.com/knowledge-centre/nifty-50-historical-returns).

### Inferences (arithmetic on the above; the 2-4% daily stock vol premise is adopted as given)
- **5-session pick distribution.** Daily stock vol 2-4% ⇒ 5-day total σ ≈ √5 × 2-4% ≈ **4.5-9.0%**. Index-relative (residual) σ removes the common factor: with average pairwise cross-correlation ρ̄ ≈ 0.3-0.5 among large caps, residual σ ≈ √(1−ρ̄) × total ≈ 0.71-0.84× ⇒ **2.5-7.6%, central case ~4-5% per 5 sessions**. Equivalent annualized-idio-vol form: residual σ₅d ≈ η_annualized × √(5/252) = η × 0.141 ⇒ η = 18-35% gives 2.5-5.0%; η = 40% gives 5.6%.
- **What a random ranker earns (the null benchmark).** Pure-random top-N picks draw from the marginal residual distribution N(0, σ_p²): hit rate = 50% by construction, expectancy = 0 − c, and conditional half-means are avg win ≈ +0.8σ_p, avg loss ≈ −0.8σ_p (φ(0)/0.5 = 0.798). At σ_p = 4%: **avg win ≈ +3.2%, avg loss ≈ −3.2%**; at 5%: ±4.0%. So the empirical win/loss asymmetry expected from a slightly-better-than-random ranker is nearly none — the payoff ratio stays ≈1.0-1.05 until e₀/s rises well past 0.1 (worked below).
- **Worked Example 1 (symmetric payoffs, cost hurdle).** W = L = 4%, c = 0.4%: `E = 8p − 4.4`. Hit rate 50% → **E = −0.4%**/trade; 52% → −0.24%; 53% → −0.16%; **breakeven p* = 4.4/8 = 55.0%**; 57% → +0.16%; 60% → +0.40%. Direct corollary: a pick with μ = +0.4% on σ = 5% implies hit rate p = Φ(0.08) = 53.98% with conditional magnitudes W = 4.14%, L = 3.85%; gross E = 0.5319×4.138 − 0.4681×3.846 = +0.40% ⇒ net ≈ 0 — the two frames reconcile exactly: **~54% hit rate ≈ net-zero on a 5%-vol pick; 55% net-zero at ±4% payoffs**.
- **Worked Example 2 (asymmetry helps, but modestly).** A book that cuts losers (stops/target geometry) might run W = 5%, L = 3.5%, c = 0.4%: `E = 8.5p − 3.9`, breakeven p* = 45.9%; at p = 50%, E = +0.35%. Practitioner breakeven tables show the same trade-off in reverse (payoff 1.5 needs only 40% wins, payoff 1.0 needs 50%) — [SpreadSheetsHub](https://spreadsheetshub.com/blogs/articles/trading-metrics-that-matter-expectancy), [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/risk-reward-ratio-trading-expectancy-relationship). But relative to the truncated-normal frame above, a fixed-payout geometry is not free: it is a reshaping of the same residual distribution, and fat left tails (5-day stock crashes exceed stops) keep realized L above plan.
- **Worked Example 3 (IC → expectancy → net).** e₀ = IC × σ_p with σ_p = 4%: IC = 0.03 → e₀ = 0.12% → net −0.28%; IC = 0.05 → 0.20% → net −0.20%; IC = 0.10 → 0.40% → net 0.00 (breakeven); IC = 0.13 → +0.12% net. At σ_p = 5.7%: breakeven IC = 0.4/5.7 ≈ **0.07**. So the cost hurdle demands IC ≈ **0.07-0.10** on this book — i.e., correlation between forecast and realized 5-day excess return of ~7-10%. The AnalystPrep calibration example uses monthly IC = 0.04 — [AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/state-and-interpret-the-fundamental-law-of-active-portfolio-management-including-its-component-terms-transfer-coefficient-information-coefficient-breadth-and-active-risk-aggressiveness); the practitioner world treats IC 0.02-0.05 as a working real-world signal and 0.10+ as exceptional (Grinold & Kahn's standard calibration; the fundamental-law pages above operationalize the same scale).
- **20-session horizon.** σ scales √(20/5) = 2× (s ≈ 8-12%), but the forecast IC usually decays with horizon, so e₀ does not rise proportionally; per-trade t-stat = e/s therefore roughly halves vs the 5-session book unless the 20-day IC is at least ~equal to the 5-day IC. Treated as inference (alpha decay at longer horizons is standard in the momentum/degradation literature; the McLean-Pontiff decay evidence below is the citable anchor for the general direction).

### Gaps
- No public, India-specific 5-session cross-sectional dispersion series (CSAD/CSV for NIFTY-100 constituents with a URL) was found; the India numbers above are index-vol context plus arithmetic on the stated 2-4% daily vol premise. An NSE/dispersion-vendor subscription would close this.
- Exact normal-vs-fat-tail shape parameters (skew/kurtosis) of 5-day excess returns for Indian large caps were not found in a public source; fat tails raise s and lengthen required samples (see Key Question 2 and the MinTRL higher-moment term).

## Key Question 2: Sample-size math — how many settled trades to know the edge isn't luck?

### Takeaway
The workhorse rule for t ≈ 2 is **n ≈ (2s/e)² settled trades**: detecting 0.5%/trade at s = 4% needs ≈324 trades (about 13 months at 6 picks/week), while 0.2%/trade needs ≈1,600 trades at s = 4% (about 5 years). For hit-rate precision, ±3pp on a 55% hit rate at 95% confidence needs n ≈ 1,056 trades; ±10pp only ≈95. Correlated/overlapping picks inflate nominal trade counts — effective sample size is smaller.

### Formulas (with sources)
- t-statistic of a mean edge: `t = e / (s/√n)` ⇒ requiring t ≥ 2 gives `n ≥ (2s/e)²` (algebraic rearrangement of the t-ratio; standard inference).
- Normal-approx binomial CI: margin `m = z·√(p(1−p)/n)` with z = 1.96 at 95% ⇒ `n = z²·p(1−p)/m²`; and the margin-of-error reference points computed by practitioners: 100 trades → ±9.8pp, 400 → ±4.9pp at p = 50% — [PineConnector, "How many trades makes results statistically significant"](https://www.pineconnector.com/blogs/pico-blog/how-many-trades-backtest-sample-size); "≈96 independent trades estimate win rate within ±10% at 95%; ≈384 trades for ±5%" — [Algorier, "Minimum Number of Trades for a Backtest"](https://algorier.com/blog/minimum-number-of-trades-for-a-backtest); 30 trades → ±17.9pp — [PineConnector](https://www.pineconnector.com/blogs/pico-blog/how-many-trades-backtest-sample-size).
- The "30 trades" cliché is explicitly de-anchored by practitioners: it is a CLT rule-of-thumb, not adequacy — [Medium/Trading Dude guide](https://medium.com/@trading.dude/how-many-trades-are-enough-a-guide-to-statistical-significance-in-backtesting-093c2eac6f05), [PineConnector](https://www.pineconnector.com/blogs/pico-blog/how-many-trades-backtest-sample-size), [BacktestBase ("most strategies need 200-500 trades covering multiple regimes")](https://www.backtestbase.com/education/how-many-trades-for-backtest).
- Probabilistic-Sharpe version (equivalently "how many trades until SR>0 is 95% credible"): PSR = Φ( (SR̂ − SR*)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃SR̂ + (γ₄−1)/4·SR̂²) ) — [Quantdare PSR explainer](https://quantdare.com/probabilistic-sharpe-ratio), [QuantConnect PSR research note](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/17112/probabilistic-sharpe-ratio); Minimum Track Record Length `MinTRL = 1 + (1 − γ₃SR̂ + (γ₄−1)/4·SR̂²)·(Z_α / (SR̂ − SR*))²` — [López de Prado QWAFAFEW slides, "The Sharp Razor" (with IID-Normal MinTRL table: SR=0.5 annualized → ~11-13 years)](http://boston.qwafafew.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2017/01/Lopez_de_Prado_Sharpe.pdf), [Portfolio Optimizer (two-sample MinTRL generalization)](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-probabilistic-sharpe-ratio-hypothesis-testing-and-minimum-track-record-length-for-the-difference-of-sharpe-ratios). For normal returns and SR*=0 this reduces to `n ≈ (1.645·s/e)² + 1`.
- Shrinking effective sample: "correlated trades count as less than separate observations… five trades from one macro event may be closer to one independent sample" — [Algorier](https://algorier.com/blog/minimum-number-of-trades-for-a-backtest); likewise 100 trades from a single regime "may be worse than 80 clean independent trades" — [Medium/Trading Dude](https://medium.com/@trading.dude/how-many-trades-are-enough-a-guide-to-statistical-significance-in-backtesting-093c2eac6f05).

### Inferences (worked examples)
- **Worked Example 4 (n = (2s/e)² grid).** For e = 0.5%/1%/2% and s = 3%/4.5%/6%: e=0.5% → n = 144 / 324 / 576; e=1% → 36 / 81 / 144; e=2% → 9 / 20 / 36. At the recommended trade-precision band (per-trade SR ≈ 0.125, e.g. 0.5% at 4%): PSR-95% needs n ≈ (1.645×8)² + 1 ≈ **174 trades**; t≈2 (two-sided) needs ≈**324 trades**.
- **Worked Example 5 (binomial hit-rate precision).** p = 0.55, m = ±3pp at 95%: `n = 1.96²·(0.55·0.45)/0.03² = 3.8416×0.2475/0.0009 ≈ 1,056`. For ±5pp: ≈ 380. For ±10pp: ≈ 95. Sanity anchors match published practitioner numbers (96→±10pp, 384→±5pp at p=0.5) — [Algorier](https://algorier.com/blog/minimum-number-of-trades-for-a-backtest).
- **Worked Example 6 (production rate).** ~6 settled picks/week × 52 = **~300 settled picks/year** (min ~26×6=156 at 6/month, up to ~360 at 30/month). At 5-session holds that is ~6 concurrent slots; at 20-session holds, ~24 concurrent slots for the same entry rate. Consequences: mean-edge detection at e=0.5%, s=4% → t = 0.5×√300/4 = **2.17** after one year (just detectable); e=0.3% → t=1.30; e=0.2% → t=0.87 (invisible); hit-rate ±3pp precision needs ≈1,056 picks ⇒ **~3.5 years** of 6/week output.
- Multiple-regime coverage caveat: 200-500 trades is the community's working minimum and trades clustered in one regime are one effective data point — [BacktestBase](https://www.backtestbase.com/education/how-many-trades-for-backtest), [Arrow Algo ("300 trades in one bull market is still one data point in the way that matters")](https://arrowalgo.com/backtest-sample-size).

### Gaps
- No canonical citation found for the exact "(2s/e)²" mnemonic; it is presented here as the algebraic rearrangement of t = e/(s/√n), consistent with the t-tests and CI arithmetic in every practitioner source above.
- Nominal-to-effective sample adjustment factors for overlapping 5-20 day holds are firm-specific and unpublished; treat "at least halve your nominal trade count when entries share a common evening signal and regime" as a conservative practitioner inference, not a measured constant.

## Key Question 3: How professionals gate promotion — walk-forward/OOS, paper→live scaling, minimum samples, preregistration, DSR/PSR/PBO, alpha decay, capacity

### Takeaway
The professional ladder is: preregister the hypothesis and log every trial → backtest with costs → walk-forward/CSCV-style out-of-sample validation with all trials counted → deflated-Sharpe or multiple-testing-haircut acceptance (DSR, Harvey-Liu-Zhu t > 3.0) → paper-trade with pre-declared pass criteria (3-6 months typical, some 50+ paper trades) → go live with small capital and scale gradually with pre-declared kill criteria. Cross-checks on any discovered edge: published edges are 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower after publication; capacity and cost drag cap the book long before sample size does.

### Cited findings
- **Walk-forward optimization** (Pardo's method): optimize on an in-sample window, test frozen parameters on the next out-of-sample window, roll forward; "gold standard" of strategy validation — [Wikipedia, Walk forward optimization (cites Pardo, *Design, Testing and Optimization of Trading Systems*, 1992/2008)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_forward_optimization), [QuantInsti walk-forward-optimization explainer](https://blog.quantinsti.com/walk-forward-optimization-introduction), [TradeStation WFA help (records OOS rate-of-profit and enables precise IS-vs-OOS comparison)](https://help.tradestation.com/10_00/eng/tswfo/topics/about_wfo.htm).
- **Multiple-testing acceptance thresholds.** HLZ: ≥316 tried-and-published cross-sectional factors ⇒ a *new* factor should clear **t > 3.0** (p ≈ 0.5-1% single-test), not the classical 2.0 — [NBER WP 20592 abstract/PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20592/w20592.pdf), [Foxholm summary (thresholds ~3.0, rising over time)](https://foxholm.com/q/research/harvey-liu-zhu-cross-section), [OpenSourceQuant R-view of HLZ multiple-testing procedures (Bonferroni/Holm/BHY; benchmark t ≈ 3.18 when unpublished trials counted)](https://opensourcequant.wordpress.com/2016/11/17/r-view-backtesting-harvey-liu-2015). Harvey & Liu's *Backtesting* gives haircut Sharpe ratios (turning observed SR into a multiple-testing-adjusted SR) and advises merging in-sample multiple tests with OOS splits — [Harvey & Liu, Journal of Portfolio Management 2015, Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF) (Exhibit 3 haircuts across sample sizes 120-1000 obs, vol 5-15%, 300 tests); republished at [CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/backtesting.pdf) and summarized at [Man Group Insights (Sep 2026 listing)](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting).
- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)** — probability true SR beats the expected-maximum-luck hurdle given N trials and non-normal returns; hurdle uses Euler-Mascheroni max-Z approximation `E[max SR̂] ≈ μ + σ[(1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))]` with γ = 0.5772; the paper's own worked illustration shows that after **1,000 independent backtests the expected max Sharpe is ≈3.26 even if every true strategy has SR = 0** — [Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio", JPM 2014 (author PDF with Python getExpMaxSR)](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf), [Quantdare DSR explainer (3.26 after 1,000 trials)](https://quantdare.com/deflated-sharpe-ratio-how-to-avoid-been-fooled-by-randomness), [MetricGate DSR methodology page (Mertens SE formula incl. skew/kurtosis terms; DSR = Φ((SR̂−SR₀)/SE); PSR is the N=1 special case; N=10 collapses PSR 0.966 → 0.629)](https://metricgate.com/docs/deflated-johnson-sharpe-ratio), [CFA Digest summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/sharper-sharpe-ratio). A 2025 framework paper organizes DSR into three implementations (DSR-L location, DSR-LS location+scale) — [López de Prado LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lopezdeprado_quantitativefinance-assetmanagement-machinelearning-activity-7500895008494018560-vJBP).
- **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) via CSCV:** split the per-strategy return matrix into S=16 chunks, evaluate all C(16,8) = 12,870 in/out-of-sample pairings; PBO = probability the in-sample winner ranks below median out-of-sample; needs ≥10 variants, ideally 50+ (typical parameter sweeps 50-200); PBO < 0.5 means selection beats chance — [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (author PDF)](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf), [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253), [Keel PBO explainer (2026)](https://usekeel.io/learn/probability-backtest-overfitting), [CRAN 'pbo' package README (Omega-ratio implementation)](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pbo/readme/README.html).
- **Preregistration/registry — when it helps.** The DSR/haircut tests are only computable if the number and vector of *all* trials is known — i.e., logging every prompt/model/universe variant is what makes DSR meaningful; Harvey-Liu recommend keeping all in-sample test counts (their FWER/FDR machinery consumes them) and merging lenient in-sample cutoffs (p≈0.2) with OOS survival — [Harvey & Liu Backtesting PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF), [HLZ PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20592/w20592.pdf). The methodological need for registries follows directly from the mixture-model setup (with probability p₀ a strategy is from the null; unknown trial counts break the correction) — [CME *Backtesting* reprint](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/backtesting.pdf). Inference: preregistration helps exactly when your research process is *iterative and high-trial-count* (LLM prompt/model sweeps are the extreme case) and is cosmetic when a single hypothesis is fixed in advance and traded untouched.
- **Paper → live → scale ladder (public practitioner/institutional norms).**
  - "Paper trade for at least 3 months before deploying real capital. Live results that diverge from backtest are information — investigate before scaling up" — [QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/articles/getting-started-paper-trading).

  - "Aim for at least 50 trades [in paper] to evaluate execution accuracy and fill speeds; most experts recommend paper trading for one to three months before going live" — [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/quantitative-trading-explained).
  - "Paper trade… at least a few weeks… then go live with small capital and scale up gradually; significant live-vs-backtest divergence usually means something is wrong" — [Quantt, "Quant Trading 2026: Complete Guide"](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/quant-trading-complete-guide).
  - Gating criteria should be defined *before* the paper run ("at least 40-60 paper trades across volatility conditions, slippage within tolerance, no order failures, drawdown below validated OOS max; a strategy good in one quiet month has not earned production capital") — [TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/backtesting-market-regimes-for-2026-strategies).
  - Decide in advance what evidence switches a strategy off (the practitioner "step 12": kill-switch criteria; live-vs-paper Sharpe gap is usually execution plus signal half-life) — [Mehul Mehta, LinkedIn systematic-trading-steps thread](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mehul-mehta4_how-do-quant-hedge-funds-actually-build-systematic-activity-7516713597482270720-rbFq).
- **Alpha decay / publication effects.** For 97 academic cross-sectional predictors: portfolio returns are **26% lower out-of-sample** (upper bound of data-mining) and **58% lower post-publication**; the extra 32pp is informed-trading decay. Averages: in-sample 0.582%/month → OOS 0.402% → post-publication 0.264%; declines are larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns, and decay continues linearly (~−9.5% more per 100 months) — [McLean & Pontiff, *Journal of Finance* 2016 (Wiley abstract)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365), [author PDF](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/AcademicReviewFactor.pdf), [CFA Digest with the concrete monthly-data numbers](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary). Post-publication correlation among published predictors rises (crowding) — [same paper's FRM/BoCconi working-paper version](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/Jeffrey-Pontiff.pdf). Practitioner rotation guidance: extract ideas from working papers before publication since journal lag is 1-2 years, and rotate away from crowded anomalies — [QuantPedia, "How do investment strategies perform after publication?"](https://quantpedia.com/how-do-investment-strategies-perform-after-publication) — same effect claimed for "new quant" strategies in industry literature — [With Intelligence "Next Generation Quant" whitepaper 2025 ("many financial strategies experience diminishing returns after they become public knowledge")](https://www.withintelligence.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/next-gen-quant-white-paper.pdf).
- **Capacity/drag.** Impact cost grows with order size and is a first-class cost line: NSE's working paper puts total explicit cost ~33bps on ₹1-lakh orders (incl. ~6bps impact) and multiples on larger orders — [NSE WP 2](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSEWP_2.pdf); the canonical capacity anecdote is Renaissance's Medallion closing to external capital in 1993 precisely because elite strategies scale poorly — [Quantt guide](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/quant-trading-complete-guide). Indian delivery frictions beyond brokerage are unusually high (STT-heavy) and policy-sensitive — [ICRIER WP 273](https://icrier.org/pdf/working_paper_273.pdf), [Nithin Kamath LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nithin-kamath-81136242_securities-transaction-tax-stt-is-the-biggest-activity-7359153100898582528-oFd7).

### Inferences
- **Worked Example 7 (DSR-style hurdle for a 1-year solo/LLM book).** n = 300 settled trades in the year, s = 4% ⇒ SR̂ sampling noise under the null ≈ 1/√300 = 0.0577 (per-trade SR units of e/s). Expected max SR̂ across N trial variants: N=10 → maxZ = 0.4228×1.2816 + 0.5772×1.7915 = 1.576 ⇒ E[max] = 0.0910 (e ≈ 0.36%); N=100 → maxZ = 0.4228×2.326 + 0.5772×2.677 = 2.529 ⇒ E[max] = 0.1464 (e ≈ 0.59%). Punchline: an observed per-trade SR of 0.125 (e = 0.5%, s = 4%) is **below** the expected-maximum from 100 prompt/model variants tried on pure noise — i.e., unregistered trial counts of ~100 fully explain the result. All maxZ arithmetic from the [Bailey & López de Prado DSR paper formula](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).
- **Worked Example 8 (decay haircut applied to a real edge).** If an un-crowded but well-known signal family retains ~74% of in-sample performance out-of-sample and ~42% post-publication (McLean-Pontiff's 26%/58% declines — [JOF abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)), a private, not-yet-public variant sitting between the two might expect ~0.6-0.8 × the backtest edge; a 0.5%-per-trade tested edge becomes ≈0.37-0.40% live OOS, which just barely beats the 0.4% cost hurdle — i.e. private edges near the cost hurdle die entirely after decay+costs.
- **Where gates actually bite (integration of the above).** The ladder (log trials → OOS/walk-forward → DSR/PBO → paper criteria → small live → scale) is internally consistent with the sample-size math: paper trading 3-6 months at 6 picks/week yields ~78-156 settled picks, which (Worked Examples 4-6) can detect only e ≥ ~0.7-1.0%/trade claims or catastrophic negatives — so the paper gate is a *bug-and-execution* gate plus a coarse sanity gate, and the statistical burden stays on the DSR/multiple-testing layer.

### Gaps
- Firm-specific, public-by-name gating rules (e.g., exact AQR/Man/Indian-desk promotion-rung sizes) are not published; the above is the best public practitioner+academic composite. Direct institutional rulebooks are career-sensitive and undocumented.
- "Registry/preregistration" has no finance-specific registry platform found in these searches (clinical-trial-style registries exist for other fields); the claim that preregistration *helps* in trading research is a reasoned consequence of the DSR/multiple-testing mathematics rather than an evaluated field experiment.

## Key Question 4: The honest frame — what edge is DETECTABLE in a year at 6-30 picks/month, and what does it imply for a daily-evening news+technicals LLM forecast book (5/20-session, long-only)?

### Takeaway
In one year (72-360 settled picks), per-trade edges below ~0.5-1.4% are statistically invisible at t≈2, while the realistic edge from a competent (IC≈0.03-0.05) long-only 5-session pick is ~0.1-0.25% — *below* the ~0.4% cost hurdle, let alone detectability. Therefore: (a) the book's one-year verdict can only rule out bets bigger than reality (e.g., it cannot distinguish +0.3% edge from zero); (b) accepting the 0.4% hurdle requires hit rate ≥ ~54-55% at ~±4% payoffs or IC ≥ ~0.07-0.10 — elite for this signal class; (c) a news+technicals LLM book is structurally a multi-trial research process (prompt/model/universe variants), so unlogged trial counts multiply the honest hurdle via DSR; and (d) feasibility lives in breadth and scalability: scale to many slots per evening, cut costs (payoff geometry, cheaper execution horizons), and treat the first year as an instrumented measurement experiment whose only publishable twin hypotheses are "≥0.4-0.5% net per trade" and "does not exist."

### Cited findings
- Detection thresholds vs production rates: edges need 200-500+ trades minimum, 100-trade records leave "massive margin of error… apparent edge could easily be pure statistical noise" — [BacktestBase](https://www.backtestbase.com/education/how-many-trades-for-backtest), [FXNX (high-variance breakout systems: 100 trades can yield p=0.22; 300-500 trades needed for p<0.05 at intraday/forex cadences)](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/backtest-sample-size-how-many-trades-make-results-real), [EdgeFlo (at 10-trades samples a 45% true win rate can print anywhere from 10% to 80%)](https://www.edgeflo.com/blog/sample-size-trading).
- Multiplicity and trial-count honesty: DSR/PBO/haircut literature as in Key Question 3 ([Bailey paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf), [Keel PBO explainer](https://usekeel.io/learn/probability-backtest-overfitting), [Harvey & Liu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)); decay: [McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365).
- Cost reality: [Zerodha STT explainer](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/how-is-the-securities-transaction-tax-stt-calculated), [NSE levies page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/invest/first-time-investor-sebi-turnover-fees-stt-other-levies), [NSE cost-of-trading WP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSEWP_2.pdf), [ICRIER STT WP](https://icrier.org/pdf/working_paper_273.pdf).
- News+LLM signal class risk is now topical enough to appear in the practitioner press ("mini-quant funds" run by AI agents, e.g. WSJ feature on individuals delegating stock scanning to named AI agents) — [WSJ, "He's Letting AI Agents Invest His Money. They Even Have Names." (2026)](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/the-ai-shift-turning-everyday-investors-into-mini-quant-funds-ebe4d45f). The stated design (LLM forecasts from news+technicals, evening runs) is therefore in a class with dozens of similar unregistered trials — the multiple-testing subtext is the WSJ article's existence itself, not a quantitative claim.

### Inferences (worked examples, one-year detection at the described production rates)
- **Worked Example 9 (minimum detectable edge in a year).** MDE(95%, t=2) = 2s/√n. At 6 picks/week (n≈300): s=4% → **MDE ≈ 0.46%/trade**; s=6% → 0.69%. At 6 picks/month (n=72): s=4% → 0.94%; s=6% → 1.41%. At 15/month (n=180): s=4% → 0.60%; s=6% → 0.89%. These are the honest bars: the book must *sustain* per-trade net edges of ~0.5-1.4% for its year-one result to carry statistical meaning — which is why 20-session horizons (whose s is ~2× and whose e is no larger when IC decays) make the honest bar harder, not easier.
- **Worked Example 10 (irreconcilable vs hurdle).** A "good" IC = 0.05 on picks of σ = 4-6% ⇒ e₀ = 0.20-0.30% gross ⇒ net −0.20% to −0.10% per trade after the 0.4% cost. Book-level translation: 300 picks/year at 6 concurrent slots (each slot = 1/6 of book) ⇒ annual book return ≈ (300/6) × (−0.10% to −0.20%) = **−5% to −10%/yr** before impact-cost escalation. The fundamental-law check agrees in spirit: IR = IC·√BR = 0.05×√300 ≈ 0.87 is the *unconstrained upper bound* (perfect transfer coefficient, no costs) and it rests on an IC the acceptance literature treats as elite; the same math at IC = 0.03 gives IR ≈ 0.52 — [fundamental law sources at Q1](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/state-and-interpret-the-fundamental-law-of-active-portfolio-management-including-its-component-terms-transfer-coefficient-information-coefficient-breadth-and-active-risk-aggressiveness); practitioner calibration of "SR=1 is impressive" — [Harvey's AHL slide deck, Wharton](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/3-Campbell-Harvey.pdf).
- **What one year *can* prove (instrumented experiment design).** With 72-300 picks, a one-year paper-plus-small-live run can (a) **rule out** positive edges *above* the MDE (0.5-1.4% — i.e., if it nets materially positive, that is itself surprising and itself needs DSR re-examination with logged trial counts); (b) measure realized slippage/skew (last-trading-session effects, gap risk through stops); (c) estimate the *sign stability* of hit rate across regimes (needs ≥200 regime-diverse picks, cf. [BacktestBase](https://www.backtestbase.com/education/how-many-trades-for-backtest)); and (d) collect the trial registry that makes any future DSR statement legitimate — [DSR formula requires N trials](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf). Practical acceptance gates consistent with public norms: **pre-commit** criteria (≥ ~50 paper trades, slippage tolerance, drawdown max, kill-switch evidence) before the live run — [TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/backtesting-market-regimes-for-2026-strategies), [QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/articles/getting-started-paper-trading), [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/quantitative-trading-explained) — then scale capital only after ~2-3 years / ~1,000 picks, at which sample true edges of ≈0.25-0.3%/trade (s=4%) finally reach t≈2 and the ±3pp hit-rate test (n≈1,056) is approachable (Worked Examples 4-5).
- **Bottom-line math for the described LLM book.** Net expectancy per pick must exceed +0.4% (cost); realistically it starts *negative* until IC ≥ 0.07-0.10 (or a cost-cutting edge is engineered); at IC 0.05 expected net ≈ −0.2%/trade; the year-1 t-stat of a true +0.3% edge at s=4%, n=300, is t=1.3 — **you cannot see the winning outcome you are hoping for within the year you are going to look**. Therefore the rational one-year posture for the described news+technicals book is: minimize cost per round trip, maximize regime coverage, keep a full trial registry, size small with pre-declared pass/fail criteria, and explicitly treat the year as a measurement campaign rather than a P&L campaign — expecting flat-to-negative results to be *indistinguishable* from a small winning edge unless the signal family is exceptional.

### Gaps
- No public study was found quantifying the *realized* OOS expectancy of unregistered LLM-as-analyst news+technicals books specifically; the heterogeneous literature on news/announcements exists (and practitioners use LLM forecasting in live products — [QuantInsti, Ernest Chan faculty page mentioning "Trading Using LLM" course](https://www.quantinsti.com/faculty/ernest-chan)) but no 2025-2026 aggregate acceptance/gate statistics for such books were located.
- The "registry/preregistration" position above is fully derived from the DSR/PBO mathematics; no empirical study that *measures* how much preregistration changes realized live out-of-sample expectancy of trading strategies was found in these searches.
- 20-session-horizon IC decay constants for the news+technicals signal family are unknown (forecast-decay rates are firm IP); treated above as an assumed monotone drop.
